Two Ecosystem Zones

One Arc. Chronically Undermonitored.

Zone 1 — Northern Arc

Mesoamerican Barrier Reef System

The Western Hemisphere's longest barrier reef at 700 miles. Spans Mexico's Yucatán through Belize, Guatemala, and Honduras. Most studied zone on the circuit — but survey coverage has been dormant for years in key locations.

🇲🇽 Mexico • 🇧🇿 Belize • 🇬🇹 Guatemala • 🇭🇳 Honduras

Zone 2 — Southern Arc

Extended Caribbean Coastal Ecosystem

Fringing reefs, patch reefs, seagrass beds, and mangrove systems that are even less studied. In Bocas del Toro alone, a 2020 bleaching event impacted 90% of analyzed coral — resulting in 50% loss. Almost no longitudinal monitoring exists.

🇳🇮 Nicaragua • 🇨🇷 Costa Rica • 🇵🇦 Panama

Multi-Platform Monitoring

Aerial. Surface. Subsurface. Simultaneously.

🛫

Aerial — FAA Drone

FAA-licensed UAV for coral bleaching mapping, seagrass bed assessment, mangrove canopy analysis, and annual repeat photogrammetry for structural change detection.

🥗

Surface — REEF EcoDiver

Standardized fish counts, coral cover transects, species abundance recording across all seven countries using the globally validated Reef Check protocol.

🐠

Subsurface — PADI Adv. OW

Reef wall surveys, deeper coral structure documentation, SCUBA-depth fish community assessment at tank-refill sites throughout the circuit.

📊

Data — LSS Black Belt

Longitudinal data architecture, multi-observer measurement system validation, and quality control protocols designed for seven-country deployment and academic publication.

Scientific Partners

Currently in Active Dialogue with the Reef Science Community.

Reef Check Foundation

Global coral reef monitoring network with EcoDivers in 102 countries. EcoDiver certification training underway. New coordinator and training facility established in Bocas del Toro — the expedition's southern hub.

Healthy Reefs / HRI

Smithsonian-affiliated initiative producing the biennial Mesoamerican Reef Report Card. 70+ partner organizations across the region. Introduction facilitated by MAR Fund.

MAR Fund

Primary regional coordination body for Mesoamerican Reef conservation. Responded within 24 hours of initial outreach and is actively facilitating introductions to key partner organizations.

Global Coral Reef Alliance

Pioneers of Biorock mineral accretion technology for coral reef restoration. Active projects in Panama and Honduras — both on the annual circuit.
