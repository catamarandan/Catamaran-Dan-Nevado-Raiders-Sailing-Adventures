The Expedition

What Blue Rim 5™ Is

Blue Rim 5™ is a long-running, two-season expedition aboard Shamrocket, a 17-foot Hobie Getaway catamaran, sailed by Catamaran Dan and his Shield Maiden, Cintia. Winters are spent in the Bahamas. Summers are spent on the U.S. side, from the Dry Tortugas and the Florida Keys up the Atlantic coast to North Carolina. Then the cycle repeats.

The work is the same in both seasons: get in the water. Snorkel it, dive it, and log it. Every anchorage, snorkel site, dive site, hurricane hole, waterfront bar and restaurant, and uninhabited beach pull-up gets recorded with its position, so the next mariner has something real to go on.

Alongside the logging, the expedition supports marine field research, digs into shipwreck history, films and broadcasts live from the water, and brings the music of the Rim to the waterfront venues along the way.

How It Runs

The Rules of the Rim

Blue Rim 5™ has no finish line and no deadline. A handful of rules decide how it moves.

Weather, Not Calendar

Weather windows decide when Shamrocket moves. The quality of a place decides how long she stays. Stays get extended freely — there is nothing to rush toward.

Anchor, Mooring, or Beach

Nights are spent at anchor, on a mooring, or pulled up on a beach. Never a marina overnight unless there is truly no alternative.

Ashore for Two Things

The only reasons to come ashore are fresh water and groceries. Shamrocket needs no fuel, so the rest of the time is spent on and in the water.

Nothing Combustible

No gasoline, no diesel, no propane. Wind and a solar-powered electric motor move the boat, and solar charges everything aboard.

Log Everything

Every site visited is recorded with its latitude and longitude, conditions, and notes — built up season after season into a working register for other mariners.

Pure Snorkeling and Diving

The focus is underwater. Reefs, seagrass beds, mangrove creeks, blue holes, and wrecks — the places a 24-inch draft can reach that bigger boats cannot.

The Timeline

From Mobile Bay to the Bahamas

The first year takes the long way around — along the Gulf coast, down to the Keys, and up the Atlantic — before the crossing to the Bahamas. After that, the seasons take over. All dates depend on weather.

1Oct–Nov 2027

Departure from Mobile, Alabama

Shamrocket leaves Mobile Bay and heads east along the Gulf coast: Pensacola, Destin, Panama City, Apalachicola, Cedar Key, and Tampa Bay, then south along Florida's Gulf side toward the Keys.

2Winter 2027–28

First Winter — The Florida Keys

The opening winter is spent in the Florida Keys, from the island chain out to Key West and the Dry Tortugas. The first season of logging starts here: reefs, anchorages, and dive sites along the Keys.

3Summer 2028

First Summer — Up the Atlantic

From the Dry Tortugas and Key West, up the Florida Atlantic coast, past Georgia and the Carolinas, to Cape Lookout, North Carolina, and the wrecks of the Graveyard of the Atlantic.

4Late Oct 2028

The Crossing

After working back south, Shamrocket crosses the Gulf Stream from Fort Lauderdale to the Bahamas on a proper weather window.

5Winter 2028–29

First Bahamas Winter

The first full winter in the Bahamas — at least six months, based around Great Exuma, working the islands and cays and beginning field research with marine research teams.

6Every Year After

The Rhythm

Bahamas winters. Florida and Atlantic summers. Each season adds to the register, the research, and the story.

🇧🇸 Winter • The Bahamas

Great Exuma and the Whole Chain

Every winter from 2028–29 on is spent in the Bahamas — at least six months a season. The base is Great Exuma, home to the warmest water in the chain. From there, Shamrocket works outward across the islands: reefs, seagrass beds, mangrove creeks, tidal creeks, blue holes, and the uninhabited and abandoned cays that bigger boats pass by.

The Bahamas Register

The goal is a complete, position-tagged record covering the entire Bahamas, built one season at a time.

Field Research — GROAN™

The Bahamas winters are research seasons. Shamrocket and her instruments serve as the field platform — MOOP, the Marine Observation and Operations Platform — working alongside established marine research teams and under their scientific direction. The Bahamas work draws on five GROAN™ modules:

GRINReef

Coral reef observation and survey support.

GSINSeagrass

Seagrass bed observation.

GMINMangrove

Mangrove creek and shoreline observation.

GMAINMarine Animal

Marine-life observation, including invasive lionfish.

GEINEstuarine

Tidal creeks, blue holes, and brackish areas.

Promoting the Bahamas

The winter season is also about giving back to the islands that host it. The aim is to promote the Bahamas to boaters and divers — through the register, the filming, and live music, including concerts featuring local Bahamian bands.

What Summer Is For

Logging the coast. Every dive spot, snorkel spot, anchorage, waterfront bar and restaurant, and uninhabited beach pull-up from the Dry Tortugas to North Carolina gets recorded with its position.

Hurricane holes. Known hurricane holes along the route are checked and verified, and new ones are logged.

Shipwreck history. Summers bring research into the wrecks along the route — historical records and archives ashore, then diving the sites. From the Keys to the Graveyard of the Atlantic, this is slow, patient work done at the boat's own pace.

Research teams. Shamrocket and her gear are available to work with marine research teams on the U.S. side as well.

Filming and live broadcasts. Footage from the water and live broadcasts over Starlink, whether at anchor or from a marina.

Music on the waterfront. Songwriting along the way, and performances at waterfront venues — while promoting the Bahamas to U.S. boaters and divers ahead of each winter.

🇺🇸 Keys & Atlantic • Summer

For Mariners

Hurricane Holes, Verified

Both seasons include a job that serves every small-boat sailor who comes after: checking the location and conditions of known hurricane holes in the Bahamas and along the Dry Tortugas to North Carolina route, and logging new ones found along the way. That information is being saved and will be made available to mariners.

The Vessel

Shamrocket — Built for This

Hobie Getaway catamaran. 17 feet. 24-inch draft. Open trampolines. She gets into places bigger boats can't go — inside the reef, up the mangrove creek, onto the beach. Wind and a solar-powered electric motor move her, and a DRAGR Magnum Jet Sled carries the gear. Over 1,250 miles have already been sailed in the catamaran-and-sled configuration.
