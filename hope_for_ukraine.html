Who's actually receiving this

Hope For Ukraine (HFU) is a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit headquartered in Roseland, New Jersey, with a working office in Lviv-Vynnyky. Founded in 2016 and led by CEO Yuriy Boyechko, it has spent the war years moving food, medical supplies, and emergency support directly to the people who need them — not funding theory, funding delivery.

Where a gift actually goes

HFU runs seven standing programs. This is the honest breakdown — no industrial-development spin, just what they told me they're funding.

Family Support

Direct aid to households who lost income, housing, or a provider to the war.

Medical Support

Supplies and equipment to hospitals and clinics still operating near the front and in rebuilt facilities.

Frontline Project

First-aid kits and emergency provisions for communities closest to active fighting.

Refugee Project

Housing assistance and medical care for people displaced inside and out of Ukraine.

A Child's Smile

Ongoing care for children, including kids living with HIV.

Solar Energy Resilience

Backup power for facilities that lose the grid to strikes on infrastructure.

Hope Pets

Support for the animals families won't leave behind.

Why a sailor from a landlocked ranch is doing this

This isn't a cause I found to fit an expedition. I have immense respect for Ukrainian culture, for the Ukrainian people, and for what the country is enduring right now — and that respect is what moved me to reach out and ask what I could do. The expedition is simply the platform I have to offer.

Blue Rim 5 launches from somewhere on the Gulf Coast in the Fall of 2027 — three back-to-back expeditions covering 31 countries and territories over 1,302+ days minimum, tracing the Bahamas and Eastern Caribbean, the full Western Caribbean, and the Pacific coast of the Americas. It's built to draw press, sponsors, and an audience who wouldn't otherwise be looking at Ukraine. I intend to use every stop, every interview, and every piece of footage to keep Hope For Ukraine's name and their donation link in front of people — and that commitment will extend well after this expedition ends.

The plan doesn't stop at the last stop of Rim Run Pac. Once all three legs are closed out, the current thinking is to break Shamrocket down, crate her and the gear into a shipping container, and send everything to a Scandinavian port. From there I'll reassemble the boat and follow the coastlines of Europe south into the Mediterranean — and when conditions on the ground allow it, sail up the Dnipro River to Kyiv itself, to say hello to the people this whole effort has been about.

If your company wants its name attached to that visibility — not to me, to the cause — reach out and we'll talk about what that looks like.
