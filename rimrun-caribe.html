The Mesoamerican Reef

Arizona Goes to the Second Largest Reef on Earth.

Arizona doesn't have a coastline. But it has Catamaran Dan — and on the Rim Run Caribe, Arizona goes to the Mesoamerican Reef.

The Mesoamerican Reef is the second largest barrier reef system on the planet, second only to Australia's Great Barrier Reef. Stretching over 700 miles along the Caribbean coasts of Mexico, Belize, Guatemala, and Honduras, it is home to more than 65 species of stony coral, 350 species of fish, and some of the last remaining populations of sea turtles, manatees, and whale sharks in the Western Hemisphere. It is one of the most biologically diverse and critically important marine ecosystems on earth.

It is also one of the most threatened. Coral bleaching from rising sea temperatures. Lionfish invasion decimating native fish populations. Coastal development and agricultural runoff suffocating reef systems that took thousands of years to build. Climate change rewriting the conditions that made this reef possible in the first place.

The Route Runs Through the Heart of It.

The Rim Run Caribe doesn't pass near the Mesoamerican Reef — it runs through the heart of it for months at a time. Shamrocket's 24-inch draft puts the expedition inside the barrier reef, on the reef flats, at the patch reefs, in the channels — places no research vessel or charter boat ever reaches. At water level. Eye to eye with what is actually happening down there.

Belize alone accounts for roughly 117 days on the circuit — the Blue Hole, Lighthouse Reef, Turneffe Atoll, Glover's Reef, South Water Caye Marine Reserve. Followed by the Bay Islands of Honduras, where whale sharks gather seasonally and visibility regularly exceeds 100 feet. Then Nicaragua's Pearl Cays — essentially unsurveyed. Costa Rica's Cahuita. Panama's Bocas del Toro and the autonomous Guna Yala islands.

This is not a flyover. This is sustained, extended, water-level engagement with the Mesoamerican Reef system — stop by stop, season by season, for the full duration of the circuit.

What It Is

The Caribbean Circuit. Eleven Countries. One Loop.

Rim Run Caribe is the Caribbean arm of the Blue Rim 5 expedition — a round-trip circuit from Puerto Aventuras south through seven Central American countries, into Colombia's Caribbean coast, and out to the Dutch ABC Islands of Aruba, Bonaire, and Curaçao before the return. Eleven countries. One unbroken loop. Every significant reef system on the route documented.

This is not a recreational sailing trip with scientific branding bolted on. It is a structured coastal expedition executed from a small catamaran by someone who spent years in environments where there is no margin for sloppiness and no one else to call. Every government fisheries authority is treated as a partner. Every coastal community is entered as a guest who intends to give back more than they take.

Weather Rule — Hard
No fixed schedule north of Panama. Stays extended freely — including sitting out full hurricane seasons south of 12°N. The circuit never pushes north of ~13°N between June 1 and November 30. The sea decides the schedule. The expedition respects that completely.

The Onboard Bar

4 Tides Cantina.

Every expedition needs a bar. This one was named at Turnagain Arm, Alaska — one of the most geologically dramatic stretches of water in the world, and the place where the Alaska to San Diego run found its rhythm.

Turnagain Arm got its name in 1778 when William Bligh — sailing master for Captain James Cook, later infamous for the Bounty mutiny — was sent to survey the inlet looking for the Northwest Passage. He had to turn around. Twice. He named it Turnagain in exasperation. The arm generates the largest tides in the United States — up to 40 feet — and twice a day those tides collide in the narrow channel and produce one of the largest tidal bore waves on earth: a wall of water up to 10 feet high, moving at up to 15 miles per hour, stretching the full width of the inlet. Local surfers ride it for miles. Harbor seals ride it in. Beluga whales follow.

Four tides. Two bores a day. The same water, returning. That's the bar. Named at the place where Cook had to turn around — because this expedition doesn't.

The Vessel

Shamrocket

A Hobie Getaway catamaran — 17 feet of shoal-draft, wind-powered reef machine. Twenty-four inches of draft means Shamrocket goes places no keelboat can follow: inside the barrier reef, up against the caye, into the mangrove channel, onto the beach itself. Right up to where the people are. Where the stories live. Where the real knowledge is kept.

Wind and solar only. Zero diesel. Zero carbon. The vessel is not a compromise — it is the philosophy made physical. Small boats. Real seamanship. Cultural access that larger vessels can only admire from a distance. Dive operations run on the Air Line by J Sink hookah system — one diver to 60 feet, no tanks, no compressor, no fuel logistics. Clean, quiet, deployable at every stop on the circuit.

Reef Awareness

Eyes Open. Every Dive. Every Day.

The Rim Run Caribe carries a reef awareness lens — not a science program with adventure attached, but an adventure expedition by someone who pays attention. Water conditions, coral health, species presence, human impact — all logged, all GPS-referenced, all photographed where it matters. No institutional obligations. No data commitments to anyone. Just a qualified observer with a camera, a hookah system, and decades of experience reading what he's seeing.

On every dive where local law permits, the lionfish trident goes in. All removal events documented. These reefs are worth defending. The circuit provides a legitimate, sustained mechanism to do exactly that — stop by stop, year after year, at the same sites.

The Operating Principle
Enter every government fisheries authority as a partner. Enter every coastal community as a guest. Leave both better than you found them. This is not a slogan — it is the behavioral standard the expedition is held to at every stop.
