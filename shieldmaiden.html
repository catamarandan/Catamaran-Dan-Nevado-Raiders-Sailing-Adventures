Are You the Shield Maiden?

This is not a casual question. The Blue Rim 5 is a multi-year sailing expedition through some of the most demanding and most beautiful water on earth — 31 countries, 31 flags, 1,302 days minimum. I need a partner, not a passenger. If that's you, read every word on this page.

Why

Built to Impress One Person.

People ask who this expedition is meant to impress. The answer is one person — me. Not the sailing community. Not social media. Not institutions, sponsors, or strangers with opinions about what constitutes a worthy adventure. Three decades of work — on submarines, in boardrooms, on the water, and in the kind of places that reshape how a man sees the world — have produced a value set that doesn't need external validation. The standard is internal. Always has been.

What that means in practice: the planning is meticulous, the preparation is relentless, and the execution is measured against one question — did I do this right? Not fast. Not impressively. Right. The sea is an honest judge and a poor audience. It doesn't care about the narrative. It rewards preparation, punishes arrogance, and has no interest in anyone's personal brand. That suits this expedition perfectly.

The highest loyalty here is to humanity itself — not to a tribe, a nation, a religion, a politics, or an ideology. The mission underneath every mile of this route is the advancement, protection, and survival of human civilization and the natural systems that sustain it. The Shield Maiden who belongs on this boat understands that instinctively. It doesn't need to be explained.

This is not performance. It is purpose — built from the inside out, over thirty years, and now pointed at the water.
The Mission

Three Expeditions. One Life.

This is not a sailing trip. This is not a sabbatical. This is the Blue Rim 5 — three sequential expeditions aboard Shamrocket, a 17-foot Hobie Getaway catamaran with a 24-inch draft, built to go places no keelboat ever will. Here is the full scope, unburied.

Before Anything Else

This Life Is Real.

I'm going to tell you exactly what this is before I tell you why it's worth it. That's respect, and it's how this whole expedition works.

The Blue Rim 5 is not a vacation that never ends. It is a continuous, live-aboard expedition — structured, disciplined, and purpose-driven. We sail 31 countries on a 17-foot Hobie Getaway catamaran. We anchor in places most sailors never find. We dive. We explore. We move when the weather says move and stay when the place is worth staying.

Some days are luminous. Some are long and wet and physically demanding. Some anchorages are paradise. Some are sketchy. Weather windows are not suggestions — they are decisions that determine whether you sleep or sail through the night.

The boat is small. The ocean is not. The mission is real. There is no support crew. There is no shore team waiting. At the core, there are two people on a catamaran, and both of them need to know what they're doing.

If that made you lean forward instead of back — keep reading.

"This is the greatest adventure you will ever have. I need the woman who already knows that — and is asking when we leave, not whether she can handle it."
What You Bring

The Woman This Requires.

I'm not writing a job description. I'm describing a person. If you read this list and recognize yourself, that recognition matters more than any resume.

  • 01
    Seamanship — or the drive to earn itYou don't need a captain's license. You need an honest relationship with the water. You've sailed, kayaked, crewed, fished, or dived seriously. You understand that the ocean sets the terms. If you're newer to sailing, you're not disqualified — but you need to be the kind of person who learns fast, takes instruction without ego, and earns competency through action.
  • 02
    Calm under pressureWhen conditions change — and they will — I need someone who gets quieter, not louder. Who thinks before they speak and moves with purpose. Panic is contagious on a small boat. Steadiness is more valuable than strength.
  • 03
    Physical capability and willingnessThis is not a charter. You handle lines. You anchor the boat. You dive. You carry equipment. You do it tired. You do it in rain. You do it because the mission requires it and you signed on for the mission.
  • 04
    Genuine curiosity about the natural worldThe Blue Rim 5 takes us through some of the most spectacular coastal water on earth. You need to actually care what's below the surface — to find wonder in a reef system, a whale encounter, a bioluminescent night anchorage. The ocean is the destination. Curiosity about it is not optional.
  • 05
    A compass of her ownI'm not looking for someone who follows. I'm looking for someone who navigates. You have your own values, your own judgment, and the backbone to say what you actually think. A partner is someone who challenges a bad idea before we execute it — not after.
  • 06
    Storyteller, filmmaker, social animalThe Shield Maiden owns the story. She is the public face of this expedition in every port, every restaurant, every waterfront bar across 31 countries. She films, she posts, she connects, she draws people in. She walks into a room full of strangers and comes out an hour later with three new friends and a video worth watching. Presentable, outgoing, sharp enough to read a room in a foreign country and connect anyway. This is a leadership role with a camera and a personality.
  • 07
    Comfort with simplicity and closenessThe boat is our world for 1,302 days minimum. There is no private suite. There is no off-day from each other. The woman this works for is not claustrophobic in close quarters with someone she trusts — she finds freedom in the simplicity of a life that fits in a waterproof bag.
  • 08
    A sense of humor that doesn't need comfortThings go sideways. Plans change. Equipment breaks. The woman who's right for this laughs at the absurdity of the situation before she fixes it. Not because she doesn't take the mission seriously — because she takes herself lightly enough to stay effective when things get hard.
  • 09
    Commitment to the full scopeThis is not a short passage. This is not a season aboard. This is the Blue Rim 5 — all three expeditions, 31 countries, the Caribbean and the Pacific and back again. The Shield Maiden who belongs here is not looking for an adventure. She is looking for a life.
The Music of the Rim

The expedition carries a Brazilian timbau and bongos. If the Shield Maiden brings her own musical talent — voice, guitar, any instrument — there is no reason we won't be writing and recording original music at anchor across 31 countries and sharing it with the world. Every anchorage has its own sound. Every crossing has its own rhythm. The right partnership writes the soundtrack as it goes.

Musical talent is not a requirement. But if it's part of who you are, bring it. There will be a stage at every sunset.

What You Get

The Greatest Adventure of Your Life.

I mean that plainly. Not as a sales pitch. As a statement about what this expedition actually is for the person who's right for it.

The full Blue Rim 5 — the Bahamas, the Eastern Caribbean, the Western Caribbean circuit, the Pacific coast from Panama to Victoria BC, the Galápagos, the Sea of Cortez, and the Caribbean again to stay. Every flag earned at sea level, on a 17-foot catamaran, two people.

The Living Ocean

Whale sharks in the Sea of Cortez. Bull sharks in Belize. Humpbacks off Colombia. Galápagos marine iguanas. Pacific kelp forests. Caribbean reef systems that most sailors sail over without stopping. You are not watching this on a screen — you are in it, every day, for years.

The Living Saga

The expedition is documented in real time — photos, writing, field notes, the whole arc of it. You are part of a story being told to the world as it happens. Your perspective is part of the permanent record. The Shield Maiden is not background. She is one of the two protagonists.

True Partnership

Not a hierarchy. Not a captain and crew. A two-person team where both people carry the mission and both people carry each other. That's rare. That's what this is designed to be — from Pensacola to La Paz and beyond.

No Schedule. No Deadline.

The expedition runs on good judgment, not a calendar. We don't push north in hurricane season. If a place is worth an extra month, we give it an extra month. 1,302 days is the minimum — not the ceiling.
