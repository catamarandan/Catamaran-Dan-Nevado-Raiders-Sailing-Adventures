What It Is

The Pacific Circuit. A Different Ocean Entirely.

Rim Run Pac™ begins where the Caribbean circuit ends — in Panama City, on the Pacific side. Shamrocket portages across the isthmus and enters a completely different ocean: colder, deeper, wilder, and in many stretches almost entirely unsurveyed by small-boat expeditions. The route runs south to Chile, then back north along the Pacific coast of Central America, through the Sea of Cortez and Baja, to the circuit's northern terminus.

This is not a repeat of the Caribbean experience with different water. The Humboldt Current runs cold off Peru and Chile, supporting marine life assemblages that have no Caribbean equivalent. The Sea of Cortez — called the world's aquarium by Jacques Cousteau — is a closed system of extraordinary biological richness. The Osa Peninsula in Costa Rica is where jungle meets Pacific reef in a zone that has almost nothing in common with the Caribbean side of the same country.

Same boat. Same sailor. Same methodology. Completely different ocean. That combination — consistency of platform across both circuits — is what makes the observations from this expedition genuinely worth paying attention to.

The Portage Rule
Shamrocket never transits the Panama Canal. The vessel portages across the isthmus by land — a deliberate choice that keeps the expedition independent of canal scheduling, fees, and the bureaucratic overhead of a commercial waterway transit. The Pacific circuit begins clean, on its own terms.
Why It Matters

Eight Coastlines. One Unbroken Look.

The Pacific coast of the Americas is one of the least-documented stretches of coastline on earth from a small-boat perspective. Commercial vessels follow offshore lanes. Dive liveaboards concentrate on known sites. The inshore zones — the kelp forests, the rocky reefs, the estuaries, the mangrove edges — are largely passed over. Shamrocket goes exactly there.

🌊

Sea of Cortez

Jacques Cousteau called it the world's aquarium — and he wasn't wrong. One of the most biodiverse marine environments on the planet, with a thermal and biological profile dramatically different from the Caribbean. The northern Pacific terminus for the circuit.

🐋

Ecuador Coastal Arc

Where the cold Humboldt and warm Panama Currents converge along the mainland coast. Machalilla NP is Ecuador's premier marine protected area. Humpback whales congregate here seasonally in numbers rarely seen elsewhere on the Pacific coast.

🌡️

Humboldt Current

One of the most productive ocean systems on earth. Cold-water upwelling off Peru and Chile supports sea lion colonies, penguin rookeries, and marine communities that have no Caribbean analog. An entirely different chapter of the Pacific story.

🌿

Osa Peninsula

Corcovado National Park borders one of the most biodiverse coastal zones on earth. Pacific jungle meets reef in a system almost completely distinct from Costa Rica's Caribbean side — same country, different planet.

🦈

Coiba National Park

Panama's Pacific crown jewel — a UNESCO World Heritage site with whale sharks, hammerheads, and reef systems that rival anything in the Caribbean. The circuit's southern Pacific entry point and one of the most significant marine protected areas in the eastern Pacific.

🐆

Colombia Pacific

Bahía Solano and Nuquí sit at the edge of the Chocó bioregion — one of the most biodiverse places on earth. Humpback whales calve here. The jungle runs directly to the water's edge. Almost no recreational sailing traffic. Exactly where Shamrocket belongs.
