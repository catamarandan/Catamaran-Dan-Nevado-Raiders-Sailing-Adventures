Rim Run™

The Life — Catamaran Dan

I’m at anchor in an extraordinary stretch of Caribbean water, the hulls resting over white sand and scattered coral heads, steady trades moving across a turquoise horizon. This is where my day begins.

I wake barefoot on the tramp of my Hobie Getaway, coffee in hand, reef fish already moving beneath me. The boat rocks lightly under the last breath of the night swell. First light spreads across the surface before the sun clears the edge of the sea.

Some nights I sleep right there under the open sky — stars overhead, salt in the air, wind steady and clean. Other nights, when rain rolls through, the two-second pop-up shelter goes up and I listen to the trade winds tap against fabric while the hulls settle quietly on their anchor line.

I turn my head left — horizon. Turn right — horizon. Sit up — sky in every direction. A full 360° circle of water and weather.

This is the Rim Run™ circuit — Panama to Belize and back — a yearly immersion through some of the clearest, warmest water in the Caribbean.

Panama

Escudo de Veraguas

Shelf-to-blue immersion with real edge. Light flickers across coral ridges, then the bottom falls away into deeper cobalt. On calm windows, visibility sharpens and every stroke feels charged.

Panama

Bastimentos & Bocas Micro-Reefs

Warm, protected water built for daily swims. Shallow coral heads rise from white sand, sunlight ripples across the bottom, and you settle into long, steady strokes that reset your whole system.

Costa Rica

Cahuita Reef

When clarity opens, this reef glows. Natural swim corridors guide you forward, warm water holds steady against your skin, and the reef feels calm and alive beneath your fins.

Nicaragua

Little Corn Island

Endurance-friendly immersion water. You lock into a smooth breathing rhythm, the reef stays present and textured, and time disappears as fish flicker through coral architecture.

Honduras

Roatán

Big reef structure and deep relief. You hover near walls where the blue darkens fast, drift along living edges, and feel the scale of the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef in every direction.

Honduras

Guanaja — Bay Islands

Quiet, clean immersion. Morning light cuts deep, coral throws crisp shadows, and your only soundtrack is your breathing and the soft hiss of fins moving through clear water.

Belize

South Water Caye

Classic barrier reef immersion. Spur-and-groove terrain forms natural swim lanes, visibility stabilizes in calm windows, and you can drift for long stretches over living structure.

Belize

Glover’s Reef

Atoll-lagoon snorkeling that rewards time in the water. Coral heads rise like islands beneath you, rays glide across sand, and long-distance swim days stack up fast.

Belize

Turneffe Atoll

Massive footprint and constant variety. One session is glassy and calm, the next feels deeper and more dramatic — an atoll that stays fresh day after day.

Belize

Lighthouse Reef Atoll

Summit immersion. The blue turns electric, visibility opens, and the reef edge drops away into depth. You hover on the boundary and feel weightless — pure Caribbean clarity.

Blue Rim 5™ Global Sailing Expedition

Blue Rim 5™

Blue Rim 5™ is a long-range, multi-year global sailing expedition tracing the blue edge of the planet—coastlines, islands, harbors, and river mouths reachable under sail. It moves one leg at a time with a mixed fleet of small expedition sailboats and larger guardian vessels, following a simple rule: we go where it makes sense, when it makes sense—and stop or reroute when it doesn’t.

This expedition is not a race. No leg is intended to be hurried. Progress is measured in seamanship, understanding, and human connection—not miles per day.

Blue Rim 5™ is not a cruise, not a yacht club, and not a packaged tour. Crews join for the legs that fit their lives and operate as independent vessels with shared purpose. Each captain/vessel/crew covers their own expenses (food, fuel, maintenance, fees, local activities, and personal costs). Only expenses explicitly incurred by the fleet as a whole—and agreed to in advance— are shared and divided evenly among participating vessels.

Kids and families are central to the mission—putting young people on real shorelines so they can see, first-hand, how a dangerously warming world is already reshaping life along the waterline.
