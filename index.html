Rim Run Sailing and Reef Observation Expedition

Independent, field-based data collection supporting institutional reef research.

A 10-year coastal sailing and reef/river systems exploration and monitoring expedition — structured reef observation by day, extraordinary living by night, and a story for the ages being written one memorable day at a time. Wind and solar only. Zero carbon. Zero compromise.

From the Mesoamerican Reef off Mexico, along the coral-rich coasts of Colombia and Venezuela, down to the South Atlantic Mesophotic Reef System along Brazil, then back north through the vibrant reefs of the Lesser Antilles and across the Bahamas Banks to the Bahamas, this continuous 10-year coastal sailing journey spans 40 countries and 1000s of islands, blending reef exploration, world-class snorkeling, natural air and nitrox diving, and adrenaline-filled encounters like "running with the bull sharks" into one unforgettable ocean-wide adventure.

The Expedition

One Loop. Every Year.

Anchor down — the Rim Run way

The Coastline Circuit — From the Mesoamerican Reef, the route slips south along low-lying tropical coasts and scattered cays past Belize and Honduras, then hugs jungle-lined shores through Nicaragua, Costa Rica, and Panama into the reef-dotted waters of Colombia and Venezuela. Continuing east and south, the coastline opens into wide river-fed margins before reaching the sprawling South Atlantic Reef System along Brazil. Turning north, the journey becomes true island-hopping—leaping from reef-fringed outposts through the arc of the Lesser Antilles, then across the larger, mountainous islands of the Greater Antilles, and finally onto the shallow turquoise banks and endless cays of the Bahamas.

The Route

40 Countries • Thousands of Islands • A sailing expedition never before attempted.

From Yucatán Peninsula to Brazil and back through the Caribbean to the Bahamas — ~11,500 nautical miles, multiple reef systems, 40 countries and thousands of islands in one multi-year route. The same route, stretched across five years and refined with every season. No leg is hurried, and no place is passed without respect for the people, the governments, and the coastlines that define each shore.

Life at Anchor

This is not Escape. It is the Point.

The reef work matters. The science matters. But the life built around it matters too. The evenings at anchor, the food, the weather, the stories, the people, the rituals, the silence, the music — this is not some decorative side element to the mission. It is the other half of it.

What the Mint Julep is to horse racing, Bourbon Storm™ is to the yachting community. Born on a blue-sky equatorial Pacific day in 1984 that turned dark, wet, and alive — then cleared just as fast. The recipe remained private for forty-one years. It belongs to the water and to the evenings the water gives you when you've earned them.

Glass & Rim

Heavy straight-sided rocks glass with a blue rim band. Rim mix: 2 parts coarse sea salt, 1 part sugar. Wet the outside rim with fresh lime and dip. Fill with fresh ice.

Equatorial Blue Base

1½ oz white rum • ¾ oz blue curaçao • 1 oz pineapple juice • ½ oz fresh lime juice • 1 oz coconut water • 1–2 dashes ginger or aromatic bitters. Shake hard ~10 seconds. Strain over fresh ice.

The Dark Storm Cloud

Float ½–¾ oz dark rum over the back of a bar spoon. Blue below. Dark above. Salt and sky at the rim. Do not stir.

Bourbon Storm™ Variation

Same blue base. Float ½–¾ oz bourbon instead of dark rum. The bourbon is the star — the first nose, first sip, and finish live in that top layer.

Rim Runner™ Zero

Pineapple juice, coconut water, orange juice, lime, blue curaçao syrup (NA). Float iced tea or cola as the storm band. Same glass. Same rim. Same protocol.

First drink: Face the horizon — "Better Beachy than Bitchy™"
Second: "Better beachy than busted."  |  Empty glass: one tap. "Anchor down."
