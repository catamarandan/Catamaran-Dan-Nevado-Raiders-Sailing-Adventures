▸ Lecture — Physical Oceanography: Expedition Integration
🇺🇸 English

Section 01 has built the complete physical oceanographic knowledge base underlying GROAN's GRIN module: water column structure, circulation systems, SST dynamics, wave energy, tidal regimes, current patterns, MAR geography, monitoring methods, indicators, stressors, human impacts, mitigation, restoration, comparative systems, and advanced nonlinear dynamics. Lesson 31 closes Section 01 by mapping each of these concepts directly to field operations aboard Shamrocket during Rim Run™ expeditions — translating academic framework into operational protocol.

The MOOP (Mobile Ocean Observation Platform) vessel role is the physical execution layer of GROAN's data architecture. Every Section 01 variable has a corresponding MOOP sensor, observation protocol, or data ingestion pathway. The Hobie Getaway catamaran configuration provides a stable, shallow-draft platform capable of accessing reef flat and lagoon environments unreachable by conventional research vessels — enabling in-situ measurements at the exact physical interface (reef crest, back-reef slope, lagoon) where stressor dynamics are most consequential.

SST field protocol directly implements the GRIN Source 001 pipeline built in Session 22. While NOAA/Open-Meteo satellite SST provides the spatial baseline, MOOP-acquired surface temperatures via calibrated thermistor string provide ground-truth validation at each reef node. The 7-day baseline, anomaly detection, and temporal smoothing algorithms in the GROAN SST engine are designed to accept both remote-sensed and in-situ inputs — the field measurement closes the loop against satellite products that have known coastal zone degradation from cloud cover and land contamination.

Water column profiling at each reef node captures thermocline depth, halocline position, and dissolved oxygen profile — the three structural variables that determine whether stratification is protective (suppressing mixing of cooler deep water) or harmful (trapping warm surface water). A handheld CTD (conductivity-temperature-depth) instrument deployed from Shamrocket's beam takes the profile to 30–40 m in under five minutes, generating a data record that directly feeds GRIN's stratification sub-index.

Wave and current observation during transit legs between reef nodes constitutes the Rim Run™ dataset's highest spatial value. No fixed monitoring station covers the 1,500+ nm arc from Mexico to Panama with continuous surface measurements. Shamrocket's GPS track, logged sea state (Beaufort scale + estimated Hs from visual observation), surface current estimated from COG/SOG offset, and wind direction/speed from the onboard Garmin chartplotter together constitute a continuous physical oceanographic transect — the spatial dataset that no ship-of-opportunity currently produces for this corridor.

Tidal and circulation observations at each country stopover include: anchor drag assessment (proxy for current speed), tidal height recorded against visible datum marks, and comparison of predicted vs. observed tidal timing — confirming whether local tidal dynamics match the micro-tidal Caribbean baseline or are modified by bay geometry, river outflow, or atoll configuration.

Stressor field assessment translates L25 stressor categories into visual and instrumental observation checklists: turbidity (Secchi disk depth), surface plume presence (color, extent), visible bleaching prevalence at snorkel depth, macroalgae cover estimate (belt transect), and fishing activity observation (vessel type, gear type, catch). Each checklist item maps to a GRIN sub-index input, making the MOOP protocol a direct field instrument for the GROAN decision system — not a separate citizen science exercise.

The expedition integration architecture means that every nautical mile of the Rim Run™ circuit is generating physical oceanographic data that flows upward through MOOP → GRIN → CMIE → Decision Score → DMAP-CAL™ output. The circuit is not just the expedition — it is the primary data collection instrument of the entire GROAN system.

▸ Western Caribbean Application — MAR & Rim Run™ Circuit
🇺🇸 English
  • The October 2026 Rim Run™ launch timing is operationally optimal for physical oceanography integration: October marks the tail of the Caribbean hurricane season (sea states normalizing), the end of peak thermal anomaly period (bleaching recovery observable), and the beginning of the post-spawning larval dispersal window — compressing the highest-value observational windows for SST, bleaching, and recruitment science into the first weeks of the circuit.
  • Banco Chinchorro (Mexico, first major reef node) serves as the MOOP calibration site: baseline SST, CTD profile, Secchi depth, and belt transect are established here before any subsequent node data is logged — ensuring that inter-node comparisons across 7 countries are anchored to a consistent measurement baseline rather than sequential instrument drift.
  • The Bay Islands (Honduras, mid-circuit) represent the highest-priority compound stressor assessment node: elevated nutrient signatures from mainland river discharge interact with the thermal stress regime documented in AGRRA datasets — making this the circuit node where GROAN's CMIE non-additive interaction detection is most likely to produce a Decision Score meaningfully different from a naive linear stressor sum.
  • Costa Rica's Cocos Island (final MAR-adjacent node before Panama) is outside the barrier reef system but within the Eastern Tropical Pacific upwelling zone — providing the circuit's only cold-upwelling SST data point, directly testing whether the GRIN thermocline depth sub-index correctly flags the mechanistic shift from thermal stress to nutrient delivery as the dominant physical driver.
  • The complete Rim Run™ physical oceanography dataset — 7 countries, 100+ reef sites, 1,500+ nm, zero carbon — represents the only citizen science transect in the western hemisphere that continuously covers the full latitudinal and national range of the MAR system in a single annual survey, making it structurally irreplaceable as a GROAN ground-truth validation dataset.

QUESTION 01
Why is Banco Chinchorro designated as the MOOP calibration site for the Rim Run™ circuit, and what is the consequence if this baseline step is skipped?
¿Por qué se designa Banco Chinchorro como el sitio de calibración MOOP para el circuito Rim Run™, y cuál es la consecuencia si se omite este paso de referencia?
  • A. Banco Chinchorro has the highest coral cover in the MAR and sets the biological reference
  • B. As the first major reef node, it establishes the measurement baseline before any subsequent node data is logged — without it, inter-node comparisons across 7 countries are vulnerable to sequential instrument drift rather than true ecological variation
  • C. Mexican maritime law requires all research vessels to calibrate instruments at Banco Chinchorro
  • D. Banco Chinchorro is the only site with sufficient water depth for CTD profiling
ANSWER: B · RESPUESTA: B
QUESTION 02
How does the Rim Run™ COG/SOG GPS offset method estimate surface current, and why does this transit-leg measurement have higher spatial value than any fixed monitoring station in the corridor?
¿Cómo estima el método de desplazamiento COG/SOG del GPS del Rim Run™ la corriente superficial, y por qué esta medición de tramo de tránsito tiene mayor valor espacial que cualquier estación de monitoreo fija en el corredor?
  • A. The GPS directly measures water velocity using Doppler sonar integrated into the chartplotter
  • B. The difference between Course Over Ground (actual GPS track) and Speed Over Ground vs. wind-corrected heading reveals the surface current vector — and continuous GPS logging across 1,500+ nm covers a spatial arc no fixed station network currently monitors with equivalent continuity
  • C. COG/SOG offset measures tidal height, not current speed
  • D. The method only works in open ocean; reef passages require a separate ADCP instrument
ANSWER: B · RESPUESTA: B
QUESTION 03
Why does the Bay Islands (Honduras) node produce the highest probability of a GROAN CMIE Decision Score meaningfully different from a naive linear stressor sum, and what physical oceanographic mechanism drives this?
¿Por qué el nodo de las Islas de la Bahía (Honduras) produce la mayor probabilidad de una Puntuación de Decisión del CMIE de GROAN significativamente diferente de una suma lineal simple de estresores, y qué mecanismo oceanográfico físico impulsa esto?
  • A. The Bay Islands have the highest fish biomass in the MAR, suppressing algae competition
  • B. Tidal amplitudes at the Bay Islands are macro-tidal, preventing thermal stratification
  • C. Elevated nutrient signatures from mainland river discharge interact non-additively with the thermal stress regime — producing compound bleaching at individually subthreshold stressor levels, an interaction the CMIE detects and weights explicitly while a linear index misses entirely
  • D. The Bay Islands are outside MAR jurisdiction so standard stressor indices do not apply
ANSWER: C · RESPUESTA: C
