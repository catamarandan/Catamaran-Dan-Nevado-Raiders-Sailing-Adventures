Temporal Patterns

Patrones Temporales en Oceanografía Biológica
Introductory Western Caribbean Bilingual EN · ES 3 MCQs
Lecture Text
🇺🇸 English

Temporal patterns in reef biology span at least four distinct time scales, each driven by different environmental signals and each requiring different monitoring approaches. At the diel (24-hour) scale, reef communities shift dramatically between day and night — diurnal fish species shelter in the coral matrix while nocturnal predators (moray eels, octopus, lionfish) emerge; corals extend their polyps to feed on zooplankton in the dark; parrotfish secrete mucus cocoons and cease grazing. At the lunar (29.5-day) scale, mass coral spawning events — one of the most spectacular biological synchronizations on earth — occur predictably 3–5 days after the full moon in August through October in the Caribbean, triggered by the combination of specific photoperiod, water temperature, and tidal cues. At the seasonal (annual) scale, sea surface temperatures peak in August–September across the MAR, creating the annual bleaching risk window; fish spawning aggregations occur predictably at specific locations during specific lunar phases; hurricane season (June 1–November 30) generates physical disturbance events that reset reef successional trajectories. At the decadal scale, the MAR has lost approximately 50–80% of live coral cover since 1980 — a trend punctuated by major bleaching events in 1998, 2005, and 2016 that each caused measurable step-changes in the long-term decline trajectory. Understanding which temporal scale is operating is essential for interpreting any single field observation correctly.

🇲🇽 Español

Los patrones temporales en la biología arrecifal abarcan al menos cuatro escalas de tiempo distintas. A escala diaria (24 horas), las comunidades arrecifales cambian dramáticamente entre el día y la noche — los peces diurnos se refugian en el coral mientras los depredadores nocturnos emergen; los corales extienden sus pólipos para alimentarse de zooplancton en la oscuridad. A escala lunar (29.5 días), los eventos de desove masivo de coral ocurren predeciblemente 3–5 días después de la luna llena de agosto a octubre en el Caribe, desencadenados por la combinación de fotoperiodo, temperatura del agua y señales mareales. A escala estacional (anual), las temperaturas superficiales del mar alcanzan su máximo en agosto–septiembre, creando la ventana anual de riesgo de blanqueamiento; la temporada de huracanes (1 de junio–30 de noviembre) genera eventos de perturbación física. A escala decadal, el SAM ha perdido aproximadamente el 50–80% de la cobertura de coral vivo desde 1980 — una tendencia puntuada por eventos de blanqueamiento en 1998, 2005 y 2016.

Western Caribbean Application · Aplicación en el Caribe Occidental
🇺🇸 English

The Rim Run Caribe™ circuit is explicitly designed around temporal patterns — the October departure from Puerto Aventuras is timed to catch the tail end of the coral spawning season, avoid the peak hurricane window north of 13°N, and arrive at southern circuit sites during the coolest, clearest water months of the dry season. Temporal intelligence is built into every navigation and science decision on the circuit.

  • Mass coral spawning — the October timing advantage: Caribbean coral mass spawning events peak in August–September, 3–5 days after the full moon. The October launch from Puerto Aventuras catches late-season spawning events at southern sites (Belize, Honduras, Panama) while avoiding the active hurricane season north of 13°N. CCRA observers timing their annual surveys to the 5-day post-full-moon window in August–October will catch peak larval plankton density — the critical data window for larval connectivity and reef recruitment studies along the corridor.
  • Diel survey timing — why morning surveys matter: The GROAN field protocol specifies belt transect fish surveys between 0700 and 1100 local time — the morning activity peak when diurnal reef fish are most active, visible, and countable. Surveys conducted at midday, during periods of slack or slack-like conditions, or after 1400 when fish activity drops, systematically undercount fish abundance by 15–30% compared to morning surveys. Temporal consistency in survey timing is as important as spatial consistency in transect placement for producing comparable datasets across years and sites.
  • The bleaching season and GROAN survey windows: Sea surface temperatures across the MAR corridor peak from late July through October — coinciding precisely with the period when Shamrocket is transiting from Mexico south through Belize, Guatemala, and Honduras. Thermal stress surveys — documenting bleaching prevalence, severity, and species susceptibility — are conducted at each GROAN station during this transit window, capturing the peak stress period at each latitude. By October–November when the Rim Run reaches Panama and Colombia, SST has dropped below bleaching threshold temperatures, allowing baseline (non-stressed) surveys that provide the comparison condition against which bleaching surveys are interpreted.
  • Hurricane season and the 12°N weather rule: The Rim Run Caribe™ weather protocol establishes June 1–November 30 as the period during which Shamrocket stays south of approximately 13°N — within the lower-risk zone for Atlantic hurricane tracks. This weather rule is a temporal pattern constraint built into the expedition structure: it forces extended stays at southern circuit sites (Panama, Colombia, ABC Islands) during the active season and allows northward transit only after November 30. From a science perspective, this timing constraint produces a natural experiment — southern reef sites are surveyed during the active cyclone season, capturing any storm-related disturbance effects, while northern sites (Mexico, Belize) are surveyed during the dry-season window of maximum water clarity and minimum thermal stress.
  • Decadal context for Rim Run™ baseline data: The GROAN baseline surveys conducted during the first Rim Run Caribe™ circuit will document reef condition in 2026–2028 — a period following the fourth global bleaching event (2023–2024) and preceding the anticipated intensification of thermal stress events projected for the 2030s. This temporal position makes the first-circuit baseline data uniquely valuable: it captures the post-fourth-bleaching-event condition at 37 stations across the full MAR corridor at a moment when very few systematic circuit-wide surveys exist. Every subsequent Rim Run circuit will measure change against this first-circuit baseline — building the longitudinal record that the MAR has needed for decades.

Esta restricción temporal produce un experimento natural: los sitios del sur se censan durante la temporada activa de ciclones, mientras los sitios del norte se censan durante la ventana de temporada seca de máxima claridad del agua.

Cada circuito Rim Run posterior medirá el cambio contra esta referencia del primer circuito.
Assessment — Multiple Choice Questions
QUESTION 01 / 03
🇺🇸 A CCRA observer surveys the same reef site at 14:00 and records 8 grouper per 100 m². A Rim Run™ science team surveys the same site the following morning at 08:30 and records 22 grouper per 100 m². Before concluding that fish populations have increased overnight, what temporal factor must be evaluated?
  • A. Lunar phase — grouper migrate during full moon periods regardless of time of day
  • B. Diel survey timing bias — afternoon surveys systematically undercount active diurnal fish compared to morning surveys; the difference likely reflects timing, not population change
  • C. Seasonal temperature — cooler morning water increases fish activity and survey detectability beyond what is ecologically meaningful
  • D. Observer calibration — two different observers cannot be compared without calibration regardless of time of day
✓ ANSWER: B — Diel activity patterns cause 15–30% undercount bias in afternoon vs. morning surveys for diurnal reef fish. The GROAN protocol specifies 0700–1100 surveys precisely to eliminate this bias. The apparent "increase" is a temporal artifact, not an ecological signal.
🇲🇽 Un observador de la CCRA censó el mismo sitio arrecifal a las 14:00 y registró 8 meros por 100 m². El equipo científico del Rim Run™ censó el mismo sitio la mañana siguiente a las 08:30 y registró 22 meros por 100 m². Antes de concluir que las poblaciones de peces aumentaron de la noche a la mañana, ¿qué factor temporal debe evaluarse?
  • A. Fase lunar — los meros migran durante los períodos de luna llena independientemente de la hora del día
  • B. Sesgo de cronometraje diario del censo — los censos vespertinos subestiman sistemáticamente los peces diurnos activos en comparación con los matutinos; la diferencia probablemente refleja el cronometraje, no el cambio poblacional
  • C. Temperatura estacional — el agua más fría de la mañana aumenta la actividad de los peces más allá de lo ecológicamente significativo
  • D. Calibración del observador — dos observadores diferentes no pueden compararse sin calibración independientemente de la hora
✓ RESPUESTA: B — Los patrones de actividad diaria causan un sesgo de subestimación del 15–30% en censos vespertinos vs. matutinos. El protocolo GROAN especifica censos de 07:00 a 11:00 precisamente para eliminar este sesgo.
QUESTION 02 / 03
🇺🇸 A CCRA observer at a Honduras site records unusually low coral cover (19%) in September 2028 compared to the long-term mean of 38%. September 2028 coincides with a documented thermal anomaly of +1.8°C above the seasonal maximum. What temporal pattern best explains this observation?
  • A. Decadal decline — the data point represents the continuation of the long-term 50-year downward trend
  • B. Seasonal bleaching event — the September thermal peak combined with a +1.8°C anomaly exceeded the bleaching threshold, causing acute coral mortality that registers as reduced cover in the survey
  • C. Lunar cycle disturbance — spawning activity in September temporarily reduces coral polyp visibility on transects
  • D. Diel bias — surveys conducted in September afternoons systematically undercount coral cover
✓ ANSWER: B — A single-year acute drop coinciding with a documented +1.8°C thermal anomaly during the peak bleaching risk window (Aug–Oct) is most consistent with a seasonal bleaching event. This is not decadal decline (which is gradual) nor a lunar or diel artifact (which affect fish counts, not coral cover). Post-bleaching surveys should document recovery trajectory over subsequent years.
🇲🇽 Un observador de la CCRA en Honduras registra una cobertura de coral inusualmente baja (19%) en septiembre de 2028 comparado con la media a largo plazo de 38%. Septiembre 2028 coincide con una anomalía térmica documentada de +1.8°C sobre el máximo estacional. ¿Qué patrón temporal explica mejor esta observación?
  • A. Declive decadal — el punto de datos representa la continuación de la tendencia descendente a largo plazo de 50 años
  • B. Evento de blanqueamiento estacional — el pico térmico de septiembre combinado con una anomalía de +1.8°C superó el umbral de blanqueamiento, causando mortalidad aguda de coral
  • C. Perturbación del ciclo lunar — la actividad de desove en septiembre reduce temporalmente la visibilidad de los pólipos de coral
  • D. Sesgo diario — los censos de tarde en septiembre subestiman sistemáticamente la cobertura de coral
✓ RESPUESTA: B — Una caída aguda en un solo año que coincide con una anomalía térmica documentada de +1.8°C durante la ventana de máximo riesgo de blanqueamiento es más consistente con un evento de blanqueamiento estacional.
QUESTION 03 / 03
🇺🇸 The Rim Run™ weather protocol keeps Shamrocket south of 13°N between June 1 and November 30. From a biological monitoring perspective, what temporal research advantage does this constraint accidentally create?
  • A. Southern sites are surveyed during calm, low-wind months when water clarity is highest for photography
  • B. It creates a natural temporal experiment — southern sites (Panama, Colombia, ABC Islands) are surveyed during peak hurricane and bleaching season, while northern sites (Mexico, Belize) are surveyed during the post-season dry window — enabling direct comparison of storm-season vs. non-storm-season reef condition at different latitudes
  • C. Southern sites receive more survey visits because the vessel spends more time there during the extended stay
  • D. The constraint eliminates temporal bias by ensuring all northern sites are surveyed in the same calendar month each year
✓ ANSWER: B — The safety-driven weather rule creates an unplanned but scientifically valuable temporal stratification: southern sites surveyed during active cyclone season capture storm disturbance and peak thermal stress conditions; northern sites surveyed during dry season capture baseline, low-stress conditions. This latitudinal × seasonal interaction is one of the most analytically interesting features of the multi-year Rim Run dataset.
