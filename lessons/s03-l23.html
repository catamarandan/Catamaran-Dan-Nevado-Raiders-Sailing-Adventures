Monitoring Methods

Métodos de Monitoreo en Oceanografía Biológica
Introductory Western Caribbean Bilingual EN · ES 3 MCQs
Lecture Text
🇺🇸 English

Biological monitoring programs use multiple methods in combination — no single method captures the full ecological complexity of a reef system, and each has distinct strengths, limitations, and appropriate deployment contexts. The core principle is method matching: selecting the monitoring approach whose spatial and temporal resolution, taxonomic scope, and resource requirements best match the management question being answered. Reef Check EcoDiver surveys provide standardized fish community and benthic data comparable across the global Reef Check network — the most widely used citizen science protocol in reef biology. AGRRA (Atlantic and Gulf Rapid Reef Assessment) provides more detailed coral demographic data (size frequency, disease prevalence, partial mortality) at greater taxonomic resolution than Reef Check — used for deeper research questions. Fixed-station photoquadrats generate machine-readable benthic change data at permanent GPS markers — providing observer-independent trend detection over decades. Diadema night counts, Lion Hunter surveys, and bleaching prevalence surveys are event-triggered or seasonally-timed supplements to the core protocol that capture specific ecological dynamics invisible to standard transects. UAV aerial surveys map seagrass and mangrove habitat extent, reef morphology, and surface conditions across spatial scales unachievable by in-water methods. eDNA sampling detects species presence from water-column genetic traces without requiring direct observation. Together, these methods form the GROAN/CCRA monitoring stack — a layered program in which each method contributes a distinct information type that complements rather than duplicates the others.

🇲🇽 Español

Los programas de monitoreo biológico utilizan múltiples métodos en combinación — ningún método captura la complejidad ecológica completa de un sistema arrecifal. El principio fundamental es la correspondencia de métodos: seleccionar el enfoque de monitoreo cuya resolución espacial y temporal, alcance taxonómico y requisitos de recursos mejor correspondan a la pregunta de gestión que se responde. Los censos Reef Check EcoDiver proporcionan datos estandarizados de comunidades de peces y bentónicos comparables a nivel mundial. AGRRA proporciona datos demográficos de coral más detallados. Los fotocuadrantes en estaciones fijas generan datos de cambio bentónico legibles por máquina en marcadores GPS permanentes. Los conteos nocturnos de Diadema, censos Lion Hunter y censos de prevalencia de blanqueamiento son suplementos estacionales o activados por eventos. Juntos, estos métodos forman la pila de monitoreo GROAN/CCRA — un programa estratificado en el que cada método contribuye un tipo de información distinto.

Western Caribbean Application · Aplicación en el Caribe Occidental
🇺🇸 English

The GROAN/CCRA monitoring stack is designed for deployment from a small catamaran with a single operator — every method in the stack must be executable by one person with equipment that fits on Shamrocket, works within the hookah system's depth range, and produces data that meets the corridor integration standards. This constraint is also a feature: it means the protocol is replicable by any CCRA partner site with comparable equipment and certification.

  • The GROAN visit protocol — sequence and timing: At each GROAN station visit, methods are deployed in a specific sequence designed to minimize interference between surveys and maximize data quality. Day 1 morning (0700–1100): Reef Check EcoDiver belt transects × 4 replicates. Day 1 afternoon: water quality panel, Secchi disk, photoquadrats at 5 fixed GPS stations. Day 1 evening: Lion Hunter survey (30 minutes). Night: Diadema count (25 m band transect, red-light torch). Day 2 morning (calm conditions permitting): UAV flight for seagrass and reef morphology mapping. Day 2 afternoon: eDNA sample collection × 3 depths. The sequence is not arbitrary — fish surveys must occur in the morning activity window; photoquadrats require afternoon light angles for consistent image quality; Diadema and Lion Hunter surveys are dusk/night operations; UAV requires calm morning conditions with no precipitation.
  • Reef Check vs. AGRRA — why GROAN chose Reef Check: GROAN uses Reef Check EcoDiver as its core protocol rather than AGRRA for a specific reason: global comparability. Reef Check data from a GROAN station in Belize can be directly compared to Reef Check data from Hawaii, the Red Sea, the Coral Triangle, or any of the 2,000+ sites in the global Reef Check database — enabling the MAR corridor data to be contextualized within worldwide reef status datasets. AGRRA data is geographically limited to the Atlantic-Gulf region and requires more specialized training and equipment. For corridor-level management, GROAN prioritizes comparability over taxonomic depth — the AGRRA protocol is used at selected reference sites where deeper demographic data is scientifically justified.
  • UAV as a monitoring method — the FAA certification advantage: The Rim Run™ expedition carries FAA Part 107 drone certification (Dan's existing credential), enabling legal commercial UAV operations over U.S. territorial waters and providing the documented training baseline for obtaining equivalent certifications in each of the 11 circuit countries. UAV surveys from Shamrocket's deck map reef morphology, seagrass bed extent, and mangrove canopy at resolutions of 2–5 cm/pixel — detail levels impossible with any in-water method at equivalent spatial coverage. A single UAV flight can map the full area of a reef site that would take a diver team weeks to survey transect by transect. The UAV data feeds GROAN's DMAP-CAL™ spatial mapping module.
  • The CCRA certification levels and method access: CCRA certification is tiered to method complexity. Level 1 certification (basic training, first-year observers) authorizes: Reef Check EcoDiver belt transects, photoquadrats, water quality panels, bleaching prevalence surveys, Diadema night counts, and disease rapid reports. Level 2 certification (experienced observers, second year+) adds: Lion Hunter surveys, eDNA sampling, AGRRA protocols, and data validation roles. Level 3 certification (regional leads) adds: observer calibration authority, data quality review, and network coordination. This tiered system ensures that new observers contribute immediately at the data collection level while more complex methods are restricted to observers with sufficient training to execute them reliably.
  • Monitoring frequency and the annual visit commitment: The GROAN protocol requires a minimum of one complete station visit per year for each CCRA partner site. The Rim Run circuit visits each of the 37 stations once per circuit (approximately every 1.5 years at the midpoint estimate). CCRA partner sites — local observers based near their home site — fill the annual monitoring requirement in the years between Rim Run visits, ensuring continuous annual data at every station. The combination of the annual CCRA local visit and the circuit-timed Rim Run visit produces two data points per year at stations where CCRA observers are active — the double-visit years provide calibration opportunities and validate the CCRA local data against the Rim Run science team standard.

Assessment — Multiple Choice Questions
QUESTION 01 / 03
🇺🇸 A newly certified CCRA Level 1 observer completes their first annual station visit in 3 hours, conducting only the Reef Check belt transects and skipping the photoquadrats, Diadema count, and water quality panel because "the fish data is the most important part." What is the most significant problem with this approach?
  • A. Level 1 observers are not authorized to conduct Reef Check belt transects without supervision
  • B. The monitoring stack is designed as an integrated system — each method contributes a distinct information type that the others cannot replace. Skipping photoquadrats eliminates the machine-readable benthic trend record; skipping the Diadema count removes the grazing redundancy indicator; skipping the water quality panel loses the environmental context that makes the fish data interpretable. Partial surveys produce systematically incomplete datasets that degrade the corridor integration
  • C. The fish data is not the most important part — benthic data is more scientifically valuable for reef management
  • D. 3 hours is not long enough to complete any single monitoring method reliably
✓ ANSWER: B — The GROAN monitoring stack is not a menu from which observers choose their preferred items — it is a designed system where each method fills a specific gap left by all the others. A Reef Check transect tells you about fish and benthic cover at a snapshot in time. Without photoquadrats, there is no machine-readable trend record. Without Diadema data, there is no grazing resilience indicator. Without water quality data, an anomalous fish count has no environmental context. The stack produces a complete data product only when all methods are executed together.

QUESTION 02 / 03
🇺🇸 GROAN uses Reef Check EcoDiver as its core protocol rather than AGRRA, even though AGRRA provides more detailed coral demographic data. Which scientific principle justifies this choice for a corridor-wide monitoring program?
  • A. Reef Check requires less training time, making it more accessible to citizen scientists
  • B. Global comparability — Reef Check data from MAR stations can be directly compared to 2,000+ Reef Check sites worldwide, contextualizing Caribbean reef status within global reef trends. For a corridor management program, the ability to benchmark against global data outweighs the additional taxonomic detail AGRRA provides within the Atlantic region only
  • C. AGRRA is less accurate than Reef Check for measuring coral cover
  • D. Reef Check is endorsed by more Caribbean governments than AGRRA
✓ ANSWER: B — The decision is about the scale of comparison the program needs to support. GROAN's outputs must be legible not just within the Caribbean but globally — to international funders, global policy bodies (IPBES, CBD), and comparative reef science. Reef Check's 2,000+ global sites provide that global reference frame. AGRRA's Atlantic-only coverage cannot. AGRRA is used at selected GROAN reference stations where coral demographic depth is scientifically justified — but it is not the core protocol because it cannot produce the global comparability that corridor-level advocacy requires.

QUESTION 03 / 03
🇺🇸 A UAV flight at a Belizean GROAN station maps a large area of seagrass beds showing 40% loss of shoot density compared to the previous year's imagery. The in-water belt transects show normal fish counts and benthic coral cover. What does this multi-method comparison reveal that neither method alone could show?
  • A. The UAV data is incorrect — seagrass change should be visible in the benthic belt transect data if it occurred
  • B. The seagrass loss is occurring in areas outside the belt transect zones — the UAV's large spatial coverage detected a change that the transects' limited footprint missed entirely. The stable reef fish counts suggest the reef itself is not yet affected, but seagrass loss threatens the juvenile fish nursery habitat that sustains adult reef populations — an early warning visible only through the multi-method combination
  • C. The fish counts confirm the reef is healthy — the seagrass change is ecologically irrelevant to reef fish communities
  • D. The UAV detected normal seasonal seagrass variation — a 40% density change within a single year is within expected natural variability
✓ ANSWER: B — This is exactly the type of insight that multi-method monitoring produces and single-method monitoring cannot. The belt transects sample approximately 100 m² of reef — they completely miss seagrass beds located outside the reef zone. The UAV maps hundreds of hectares simultaneously, detecting large-scale habitat change that transects are blind to. The combination reveals a spatial disconnect: the reef itself looks stable (good fish counts, coral cover normal) while the adjacent seagrass nursery habitat that feeds the reef's juvenile fish recruitment is degrading — an early warning of future reef decline invisible to reef-only monitoring.

  • C. Los conteos de peces confirman que el arrecife está saludable — el cambio en los pastos marinos es ecológicamente irrelevante para las comunidades de peces arrecifales
