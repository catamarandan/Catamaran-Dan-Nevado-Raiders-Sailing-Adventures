MAR Focus

Mesoamerican Reef — karst chemistry, multi-country attribution, and Rim Run™ operational context

Lecture
EN — English

The Mesoamerican Reef (MAR) is the world's second-largest barrier reef system — ~1,000 km of continuous coral structure running from the northern tip of the Yucatan Peninsula (Mexico) south through Belize, Guatemala, and Honduras. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, a critical fisheries resource for four nations, and the primary operational theater for the Rim Run™ expedition. Within GROAN, the MAR is the highest-priority multi-country chemical attribution challenge: a reef system chemically dominated not by ocean-scale forcing, but by karst geology and terrestrial land use across four distinct national jurisdictions.

Mexico · Quintana Roo Belize Guatemala Honduras ~1,000 km reef extent UNESCO World Heritage

Four chemical stressors define the MAR operating environment. Each requires a specific GROAN correction protocol before CMIE attribution is valid.

Stressor 01 · Karst SGD
pH ↓↓ Low DO High DIN Low salinity Ra-222 flag

The Yucatan Peninsula sits atop a vast carbonate limestone karst aquifer — the same geology that produces the region's iconic cenotes. This aquifer discharges groundwater directly into lagoonal and reef environments through submerged conduits at volumes orders of magnitude larger than typical coastal SGD. MAR karst discharge delivers low-pH, low-DO, high-DIN, low-salinity groundwater that has equilibrated with CO₂-rich soil air. The chemical result at receiving reef stations is suppressed Ωarag, elevated nutrients, and a salinity anomaly. Unlike discretionary SGD flags in other theaters, karst SGD correction is mandatory for every MAR station in the lagoon and reef flat zone — the discharge is structural, not episodic.

Stressor 02 · Agricultural Runoff
DIN ↑ SRP ↑ N:P >16 Wet season peak

Sugarcane and citrus agriculture dominate the inland watersheds of Belize and Honduras, generating elevated DIN and SRP loading that reaches MAR reef zones via river discharge and overland flow during the wet season (June–October). N:P ratios in discharge plumes regularly exceed 16, crossing the CMIE eutrophication prior elevation threshold. Agricultural runoff is superimposed on karst SGD nutrient loading, making source attribution between the two non-trivial. CMIE must distinguish agricultural surface discharge from karst groundwater discharge using stable nitrogen isotope signatures (δ¹⁵N) where available — agricultural nitrogen is isotopically distinct from karst-dissolved nitrogen.

Stressor 03 · Sargassum Events
DO ↓ pCO₂ ↑ pH ↓ (local) H₂S possible

Since 2011, the Caribbean has experienced a persistent sargassum influx driven by Atlantic nutrient loading and altered circulation patterns. Sargassum mats reaching MAR nearshore zones create acute localized chemical disturbances: decomposing biomass consumes dissolved oxygen, releases CO₂, and in extreme events produces hydrogen sulfide. These events can suppress DO below 2 mg/L and drive local pH drops of 0.2–0.4 units within hours. All MOOP nearshore DO and pCO₂ readings taken during an active regional sargassum event must be flagged as potentially confounded. A sargassum biomass index check — available from NOAA NESDIS — is required before CMIE ingests nearshore gas data during May–October.

Stressor 04 · Thermal Bleaching History
1998 · 2005 2010 · 2015–16 2023 ENSO linked

The MAR has experienced five major thermal bleaching events since 1998, each associated with SST anomalies driven by ENSO or Atlantic warm pool intensification. The 2023 event was the most severe on record for the southern MAR. Chemically, each bleaching event is preceded by a measurable carbonate suppression signal — SST rise suppresses coral calcification, reducing TA drawdown and allowing Ω to drift toward the danger zone before visible bleaching appears. CMIE uses this pre-bleaching chemical signal as an early warning input, integrating GRIN thermal stress with carbonate suppression for elevated Decision Score output 4–6 weeks before observable mortality.

Multi-country attribution rule: When a CMIE chemical signal spans reef zones across national boundaries — for example, an agricultural DIN plume originating in Belize reaching Honduras reef zones — GROAN must apply country-specific baseline corrections to each national segment before generating a cross-border Decision Score. A single unified DS for the entire MAR is not valid — national monitoring baselines, seasonal correction protocols, and intervention authority differ by jurisdiction. The Rim Run™ circuit's country-by-country sampling structure is designed precisely to maintain this national attribution granularity.

ES — Español

El Arrecife Mesoamericano (MAR) es el segundo sistema de arrecife de barrera más grande del mundo — ~1,000 km de estructura coralina continua que se extiende desde la punta norte de la Península de Yucatán (México) hacia el sur a través de Belice, Guatemala y Honduras. Dentro de GROAN, el MAR es el desafío de atribución química multinacional de mayor prioridad: un sistema de arrecife dominado químicamente no por el forzamiento a escala oceánica, sino por la geología kárstica y el uso de la tierra terrestre en cuatro jurisdicciones nacionales distintas.

SGD Kárstico: La Península de Yucatán se asienta sobre un vasto acuífero kárstico de piedra caliza. A diferencia de las señales SGD discrecionales en otros teatros, la corrección de SGD kárstico es obligatoria para cada estación MAR en la zona lagunar y la planicie arrecifal — la descarga es estructural, no episódica.

Escorrentía Agrícola: La agricultura de caña de azúcar y cítricos domina las cuencas hidrográficas interiores de Belice y Honduras. Las relaciones N:P en los penachos de descarga superan regularmente 16. CMIE debe distinguir la descarga superficial agrícola de la descarga de aguas subterráneas kársticas usando firmas de isótopos estables de nitrógeno (δ¹⁵N) donde estén disponibles.

Eventos de Sargazo: Desde 2011, el Caribe ha experimentado una afluencia persistente de sargazo. Las esteras de sargazo que llegan a las zonas costeras del MAR crean perturbaciones químicas localizadas agudas: la biomasa en descomposición consume oxígeno disuelto, libera CO₂ y en eventos extremos produce sulfuro de hidrógeno. Todas las lecturas MOOP de OD y pCO₂ costeras tomadas durante un evento regional activo de sargazo deben marcarse como potencialmente confundidas.

Historia de Blanqueamiento Térmico: El MAR ha experimentado cinco eventos importantes de blanqueamiento desde 1998. El evento de 2023 fue el más severo registrado para el MAR sur. CMIE usa la señal química de pre-blanqueamiento como entrada de advertencia temprana, integrando el estrés térmico de GRIN con la supresión de carbonatos para una salida de Puntaje de Decisión elevada 4–6 semanas antes de la mortalidad observable.

Regla de atribución multinacional: Cuando una señal química de CMIE abarca zonas arrecifales a través de fronteras nacionales, GROAN debe aplicar correcciones de línea base específicas de cada país a cada segmento nacional antes de generar un Puntaje de Decisión transfronterizo. Un DS unificado único para todo el MAR no es válido.

Application
EN — English
  • 01Karst SGD correction is mandatory for every MAR lagoon and reef flat MOOP station — it is not a discretionary flag triggered by Ra-222 anomaly detection as in other theaters. The Yucatan karst discharge is structural and continuous. Skipping the correction in the MAR will systematically understate Ωarag and overstate acidification severity in every CMIE attribution run for this theater.
  • 02When agricultural N:P signal and karst SGD DIN signal co-occur at the same MAR station, do not aggregate them into a single elevated DIN reading before CMIE ingestion. Use δ¹⁵N isotopic signature to partition the contribution of each source — agricultural nitrogen (δ¹⁵N ~8–12‰) is isotopically distinct from karst-dissolved nitrogen (δ¹⁵N ~2–5‰). CMIE requires source-attributed DIN, not composite DIN, to generate a valid intervention target.
  • 03During May–October, before ingesting any nearshore MAR DO or pCO₂ data into CMIE, check the NOAA NESDIS sargassum biomass index for the relevant reef zone. If a regional sargassum event is active, flag all nearshore gas readings as potentially confounded and assign Moderate confidence regardless of instrument calibration. Offshore MOOP stations beyond the sargassum mat boundary are unaffected and retain their normal confidence tier.
  • 04When CMIE detects a chemical signal that crosses the Belize–Honduras national boundary along the MAR, apply country-specific baseline corrections to each national reef segment independently before generating any cross-border Decision Score. Belize and Honduras have different agricultural runoff baselines, different MPAcoverage, and different intervention authority frameworks. A single unified MAR-wide DS will obscure which jurisdiction holds the intervention responsibility.
  • 05When GRIN thermal stress and carbonate suppression signals co-occur in the same MAR reef zone during August–October, generate the elevated Decision Score immediately — do not wait for visible bleaching confirmation. The pre-bleaching chemical signal precedes observable mortality by 4–6 weeks in MAR conditions. Early DS elevation is the operational value GROAN provides that single-ecosystem thermal monitoring cannot.
