Indicators

Threshold architecture — from baseline chemistry to CMIE decision triggers

Lecture
EN — English

A chemical indicator is not simply a measured parameter. It is a parameter with a defined threshold architecture — a set of values at which monitoring frequency should increase, at which CMIE should elevate the Decision Score, and at which a Critical alert is generated. Without that architecture, a pH reading is a number. With it, pH is an actionable ecosystem signal. GROAN defines four threshold levels for each primary indicator: Healthy (baseline, no action), Monitoring (increase sampling frequency), Alert (CMIE elevated DS), and Critical (High-priority intervention flag). Every value entering CMIE from L3 Source Arbitration is tagged with its threshold zone at the time of measurement.

Beyond individual thresholds, GROAN defines four derived indicators that reveal ecosystem dynamics not visible in any single parameter:

Derived 01 · pH–Ω Decoupling

pH and Ωarag co-vary predictably in normal seawater — a drop in pH should correspond to a drop in Ω. When they diverge (pH normal but Ω suppressed, or vice versa), it signals a water mass mixing event, an unusual TA anomaly, or a freshwater input with atypical chemistry. pH–Ω decoupling at any MOOP station triggers a mandatory water mass investigation before CMIE generates attribution from that station's data. Using decoupled values as independent inputs to CMIE will produce a false compound stress signal.

Derived 02 · Annual Ω Trend Rate

Absolute Ω value tells CMIE where a reef zone sits today. Ω trend rate tells CMIE where it is heading. A declining trend of >0.1 Ω units per annual Rim Run™ circuit at the same corridor site generates a CMIE trend alert — even if the current absolute value is still in the Healthy zone. A reef at Ω 3.4 declining 0.15 units per year will reach the Alert zone in less than ten circuits. Trend alerts carry a lower DS weight than threshold alerts but are operationally more valuable — they identify intervention windows before crisis.

Derived 03 · DO–pCO₂ Diel Coupling

In a metabolically active reef zone, DO and pCO₂ are anti-correlated across the diel cycle: DO peaks and pCO₂ troughs at midday (photosynthesis dominant), reversing at night (respiration dominant). Coupling breakdown — where DO and pCO₂ move in the same direction, or where the diel amplitude collapses in both simultaneously — indicates metabolic suppression. This is a compound stress indicator that must be flagged in CMIE before attribution analysis. A diel DO amplitude below 1 mg/L with elevated flat-line pCO₂ is the chemical signature of a functionally dead reef zone.

Derived 04 · Net Ecosystem Calcification (NEC)

When two MOOP stations are positioned in the same reef zone along the direction of water flow, the TA drawdown between the upstream and downstream stations quantifies Net Ecosystem Calcification: NEC = −(ΔTA / 2) × flow rate. NEC approaching zero means calcification and dissolution are balanced — the reef is not growing. NEC negative means net dissolution is occurring — the structural foundation is eroding. NEC is the earliest chemical signal of structural reef collapse and must be reported alongside Ω in any CMIE calibration record where paired upstream-downstream stations exist.

The composite stress index. GROAN's most powerful indicator is not any single parameter — it is the simultaneous crossing of multiple monitoring thresholds. When three or more primary indicators cross their monitoring thresholds concurrently in the same reef zone — even if none has reached its Alert threshold — CMIE must generate a compound stress flag. Compound stress events have historically preceded mass bleaching by 4–8 weeks. The composite index is the chemical early warning architecture that gives GROAN its decision-lead-time advantage over reactive monitoring systems.

ES — Español

Un indicador químico no es simplemente un parámetro medido. Es un parámetro con una arquitectura de umbral definida — un conjunto de valores en los que la frecuencia de monitoreo debe aumentar, en los que CMIE debe elevar el Puntaje de Decisión, y en los que se genera una alerta Crítica. Sin esa arquitectura, una lectura de pH es un número. Con ella, el pH es una señal de ecosistema accionable. GROAN define cuatro niveles de umbral para cada indicador primario: Saludable, Monitoreo, Alerta y Crítico.

Desacoplamiento pH–Ω: El pH y Ωarag covarían predeciblemente en agua de mar normal. Cuando divergen, indica un evento de mezcla de masas de agua o una anomalía de AT inusual. El desacoplamiento pH–Ω en cualquier estación MOOP activa una investigación obligatoria de masa de agua antes de que CMIE genere atribución con los datos de esa estación.

Tasa de Tendencia Anual de Ω: Una tendencia decreciente de >0.1 unidades de Ω por circuito anual Rim Run™ en el mismo sitio del corredor genera una alerta de tendencia CMIE — incluso si el valor absoluto actual sigue en la zona Saludable. Las alertas de tendencia identifican ventanas de intervención antes de la crisis.

Acoplamiento Diurno DO–pCO₂: En una zona arrecifal metabólicamente activa, DO y pCO₂ están anticorrelacionados a través del ciclo diurno. La ruptura del acoplamiento indica supresión metabólica. Una amplitud diurna de OD por debajo de 1 mg/L con pCO₂ plano elevado es la firma química de una zona arrecifal funcionalmente muerta.

Calcificación Neta del Ecosistema (CNE): La extracción de AT entre estaciones aguas arriba y aguas abajo cuantifica la calcificación neta. CNE negativo significa que está ocurriendo disolución neta — la estructura arrecifal se está erosionando. La CNE es la señal química más temprana del colapso estructural arrecifal.

Índice de estrés compuesto: Cuando tres o más indicadores primarios cruzan simultáneamente sus umbrales de monitoreo en la misma zona arrecifal — incluso si ninguno ha alcanzado su umbral de Alerta — CMIE debe generar una señal de estrés compuesto. Los eventos de estrés compuesto han precedido históricamente al blanqueamiento masivo en 4–8 semanas.

Application
EN — English
  • 01When three or more primary indicators simultaneously cross their monitoring thresholds at the same reef zone — even if none has reached its individual Alert threshold — generate a CMIE compound stress flag immediately. Do not wait for a single indicator to reach Alert level. The composite stress index has historically preceded mass bleaching by 4–8 weeks. Early compound flagging is GROAN's primary operational advantage over single-parameter monitoring frameworks.
  • 02Calculate the annual Ω trend rate for every corridor site that has been sampled in two or more consecutive Rim Run™ circuits using tidal phase-matched samples. A declining trend exceeding 0.1 Ω units per circuit generates a CMIE trend alert regardless of the current absolute Ω value. A reef at Ω 3.4 on a declining trend is a higher CMIE priority than a reef at Ω 2.8 on a stable or recovering trend — trajectory matters as much as position.
  • 03If pH and Ω diverge from their expected co-variation at any MOOP station — for example, pH reads 8.05 (Healthy zone) while Ω reads 2.3 (Alert zone) — do not use the values as independent CMIE inputs. Investigate the water mass origin first. The decoupling may indicate a freshwater mixing event, an SGD plume with atypical chemistry, or a measurement error. Using decoupled values as independent inputs generates a false compound stress signal in CMIE that cannot be corrected downstream.
  • 04Where paired upstream-downstream MOOP stations exist within the same reef zone, compute Net Ecosystem Calcification from the ΔTA between them and report it alongside Ω in the CMIE calibration record. NEC approaching zero is a structural dissolution warning that precedes Ω dropping below 2.0. It is not a redundant confirmation of the Ω signal — it is an independent early warning from a different chemical mechanism, and CMIE weights it accordingly in the Decision Score.
  • 05Assess DO–pCO₂ diel coupling at each reef zone station by comparing midday and predawn readings. If DO amplitude is below 1 mg/L and pCO₂ shows no corresponding diel cycle, flag the zone as metabolically suppressed before CMIE ingestion. A metabolically suppressed reef zone has a different Decision Score profile than a chemically stressed but metabolically active zone — the intervention target and urgency differ, and CMIE cannot distinguish them without the coupling flag.

Question 03

A) Instrument fouling — both sensors should be cleaned and the station re-sampled before CMIE ingestion / Ensuciamiento del instrumento — ambos sensores deben limpiarse y la estación volver a muestrearse
B) Moderate chemical stress — assign Moderate confidence to the station and proceed with standard CMIE ingestion / Estrés químico moderado — asignar confianza Moderada y proceder con la ingestión estándar CMIE
