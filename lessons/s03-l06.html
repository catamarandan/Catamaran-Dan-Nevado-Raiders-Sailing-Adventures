▸ Lecture — Biological Oceanography: Interactions
🇺🇸 English

Reef ecosystems are not collections of species — they are networks of interactions. The same coral cover percentage measured at two sites can represent completely different functional states depending on who is eating whom, what is competing for space, and which mutualistic partnerships are intact. GROAN's CMIE is fundamentally an interaction detector — it is built to see the signals that emerge from species interactions, not just the species themselves. This lesson maps the seven interaction classes that most directly determine reef condition and appear in GROAN's module data streams.

Competition is the most consequential interaction at the reef benthic surface — the direct contest between corals and macroalgae for space, light, and substrate. Competition operates through three mechanisms: physical overgrowth (algae growing over and smothering adjacent coral tissue), allelopathy (chemical exudates released by algae that inhibit coral growth, photosynthesis, and larval settlement within a contact zone of several centimeters), and shading (tall algae canopies reducing light availability to underlying coral polyps). Allelopathy is particularly insidious because it is invisible — a reef can appear to have moderate algae cover while the chemical inhibition zone is suppressing coral recruitment across a far larger area than the visible algae footprint. GRIN surveys must record not only algae cover but algae proximity to coral margins and coral recruitment density in algae-adjacent zones to capture this signal.

Herbivory is the critical top-down interaction that maintains the coral-algae competitive balance. Parrotfish (Scaridae) and surgeonfish (Acanthuridae) graze macroalgae continuously, keeping algae cover below the overgrowth threshold. Sea urchins — particularly Diadema antillarum in the Caribbean — are the dominant herbivores in many MAR reef zones, capable of grazing bare rock to the microalgal film that CCA requires. The 1983–84 Caribbean-wide Diadema die-off, which killed 93–99% of the population in less than a year due to an unidentified waterborne pathogen, triggered the most widespread coral-algae phase shift in recorded reef history — demonstrating that the removal of a single keystone herbivore species can drive an entire regional reef system across a phase shift threshold within months. GROAN's GRIN protocol records both fish herbivore biomass and urchin density as primary indicators.

Trophic Cascades are the indirect effects that propagate through the food web when a top predator is removed or added. The canonical Caribbean cascade runs: apex predator removal (overfishing of sharks and large grouper) → mesopredator release (snapper, smaller grouper populations increase without apex predation pressure) → herbivore suppression (mesopredators consume more parrotfish and surgeonfish) → macroalgae proliferation (herbivore reduction releases algae from grazing control) → coral-algae phase shift. This cascade spans four trophic levels and produces a reef phase shift that has no visible cause at the benthic level — a GRIN survey that records only algae cover and coral cover will not detect it. The CMIE detects it because GRIN apex predator and herbivore biomass data, combined with the benthic cover trend, produces the multi-level signal that identifies the cascade.

Keystone Species are species whose removal produces ecosystem effects disproportionate to their biomass — they hold the system in its current state. In Caribbean reef systems, three species function as keystones: sharks and large grouper (apex trophic control — their removal initiates the cascade above), Diadema antillarum (herbivory control — their removal releases macroalgae), and branching Acropora corals (structural keystones — their three-dimensional framework creates the physical habitat that the associated reef community depends on; their loss collapses habitat complexity even when total coral cover remains partially intact). GROAN's GRIN protocol tracks keystone species as a separate index because a reef that retains general coral cover but has lost its Acropora structure or its herbivore functional groups is fundamentally more vulnerable than its aggregate cover metrics suggest.

Mutualism and Symbiosis are positive interactions in which both partners benefit. The paramount reef mutualism is coral-zooxanthellae (addressed in L01 and L04). A second ecologically critical mutualism is the cleaning station — reef sites where specialized cleaner fish (Labroides dimidiatus and related species) and cleaner shrimp remove parasites from client fish. Cleaning stations attract large aggregations of fish including species not normally resident on the reef, generating localized hotspots of trophic activity, nutrient excretion, and organic matter deposition. Loss of cleaning station function — through removal of cleaner species or physical disturbance of station sites — increases parasite load across the reef fish community, reduces fish condition, and suppresses the local nutrient cycling that cleaning station aggregations generate.

Coral Disease is a host-pathogen interaction that has emerged as a major direct mortality driver across the western Caribbean, independent of thermal bleaching. Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease (SCTLD), first documented in Florida in 2014 and confirmed across most of the MAR by 2020, produces rapid tissue loss in over 20 coral species and has caused irreversible mortality at rates exceeding some bleaching events. Unlike bleaching, SCTLD mortality is not temperature-dependent — it persists year-round and continues advancing regardless of thermal conditions. For GROAN: SCTLD presence at a corridor site must be entered in the CMIE record as a distinct process signal from thermal bleaching, because the combined stressor load — active disease plus thermal stress — is non-additive and produces mortality rates substantially higher than either stressor alone would predict. GRIN disease prevalence surveys are a mandatory component of the Rim Run™ corridor protocol for all circuits from 2026 onward.

Allelopathy and Chemical Ecology extend competition beyond physical contact. Corals and algae both produce chemical exudates that influence settlement behavior of larvae, growth rates of competitors, and microbial community composition in the surrounding water. Coral chemical signals attract conspecific larvae to settled coral colonies (positive chemical recruitment cue); macroalgae exudates suppress coral larval settlement and increase microbial activity that degrades coral mucus and surface defenses. These chemical interactions create interaction zones that extend 5–20 cm beyond visible physical contact — meaning that a belt transect recording physical contact alone underestimates the functional impact of algae proximity on coral health and recruitment by a factor of 2–5×.

Los ecosistemas arrecifales son redes de interacciones. El CMIE de GROAN es fundamentalmente un detector de interacciones — está construido para ver las señales que emergen de las interacciones entre especies, no solo las especies mismas.

Competencia y Alelopatía: La competencia coral-macroalgas opera a través de la superposición física, la alelopatía y el sombreado. La alelopatía es particularmente insidiosa porque es invisible — un arrecife puede parecer tener cobertura moderada de algas mientras la zona de inhibición química suprime el reclutamiento coralino en un área mucho mayor que la huella visible de las algas.

Herbivoría: La mortandad masiva de Diadema antillarum en el Caribe de 1983–84, que mató al 93–99% de la población, desencadenó el cambio de fase coral-algas más generalizado en la historia arrecifal registrada — demostrando que la eliminación de una sola especie herbívora clave puede impulsar todo un sistema arrecifal regional a través de un umbral de cambio de fase en meses.

Cascadas Tróficas: La cascada canónica del Caribe: eliminación de depredadores apicales → liberación de mesodepredadores → supresión de herbívoros → proliferación de macroalgas → cambio de fase. Esta cascada abarca cuatro niveles tróficos y produce un cambio de fase arrecifal que no tiene causa visible a nivel bentónico. El CMIE lo detecta porque los datos de biomasa de depredadores apicales y herbívoros de GRIN, combinados con la tendencia de cobertura bentónica, producen la señal de múltiples niveles.

Especies Clave: En los arrecifes del Caribe, tres especies funcionan como clave: tiburones y mero grandes (control trófico apical), Diadema antillarum (control de herbivoría) y corales ramificados de Acropora (clave estructural). Un arrecife que retiene cobertura coralina general pero ha perdido su estructura de Acropora o sus grupos funcionales de herbívoros es fundamentalmente más vulnerable de lo que sugieren sus métricas de cobertura agregada.

Enfermedad del Coral — SCTLD: A diferencia del blanqueamiento, la SCTLD no depende de la temperatura — persiste todo el año. Para GROAN: la presencia de SCTLD debe registrarse en el CMIE como señal de proceso distinta del blanqueamiento térmico, porque la carga de estresores combinada es no aditiva y produce tasas de mortalidad sustancialmente más altas.

Ecología Química: Las interacciones químicas crean zonas de interacción que se extienden 5–20 cm más allá del contacto físico visible — lo que significa que un transecto de banda que registra solo el contacto físico subestima el impacto funcional de la proximidad de algas en la salud y el reclutamiento coralino por un factor de 2–5×.

▸ Application — GROAN Interaction Diagnostics & CMIE Routing
🇺🇸 English
  • When recording GRIN belt transect data, log algae-coral contact margin length and recruitment density in the 20 cm buffer zone adjacent to macroalgae patches — not algae cover alone. A site with 15% macroalgae cover but high contact margin density and zero coral recruits within 20 cm of algae patches is experiencing allelopathic recruitment suppression across a functionally larger area than the cover metric captures. Enter the contact-zone recruitment suppression as a separate CMIE flag. A site with 30% algae cover but low contact margin exposure and active recruitment in open zones may be less functionally impaired than its cover level suggests — the interaction geometry matters as much as the cover percentage.
  • When recording fish community data during a GRIN survey, assess herbivore functional group completeness — not total fish biomass. A reef with high total fish biomass dominated by carnivores but depleted parrotfish, surgeonfish, and urchin populations has no functional herbivory regardless of its aggregate biomass metric. Log herbivore functional group status (intact / partially depleted / collapsed) as a separate CMIE input. A herbivore functional group collapse flag elevates the phase shift proximity score regardless of current algae cover level — the system is structurally positioned for a phase shift when the next pulse disturbance removes the remaining coral cover that herbivores would otherwise have prevented from algae overgrowth.
  • When apex predator encounter rates (shark, large grouper) decline by more than 50% between circuits at a corridor site, enter a trophic cascade initiation flag in the CMIE record immediately — before any herbivore decline or algae increase is measurable. The cascade operates with a temporal lag: apex removal precedes mesopredator release by months to years, mesopredator release precedes herbivore suppression by additional months, and herbivore suppression precedes algae bloom by further months. Entering the cascade flag at the apex level, rather than waiting for the benthic signal, buys the full cascade lag time as a management intervention window.
  • When a corridor site has active SCTLD (Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease) confirmed during a GRIN survey, enter it in the CMIE record as a distinct pathogen stressor signal — separate from and in addition to any thermal bleaching entry. Never merge SCTLD mortality into a bleaching event record even if both are present simultaneously. The combined stressor load of active disease plus thermal stress is non-additive: mortality rates under compound exposure exceed the sum of individual stressor projections. The DMAP-CAL™ Decision Score for a site with both active SCTLD and thermal bleaching must be calculated using the compound stressor interaction weighting, not the linear sum of two independent stressor scores.
  • When a corridor reef site shows low juvenile coral density and moderate macroalgae cover but the algae contact margin analysis reveals that most adult corals are spatially isolated from algae patches, investigate cleaning station presence and functionality before attributing recruitment failure to allelopathy or herbivore deficiency alone. Absent or disrupted cleaning stations reduce fish community condition, suppress grazing activity, and can produce recruitment failure through a mutualism-disruption pathway that has no direct competitive or herbivory signal in standard belt transect data. Log cleaning station presence, species composition, and activity level as a GRIN auxiliary observation at every circuit — it is a zero-cost observation that closes a significant diagnostic gap in the CMIE interaction record.

▸ Assessment — 3 Questions / Evaluación — 3 Preguntas
QUESTION 01
A GRIN survey records 18% macroalgae cover and near-zero juvenile coral density. Two interpretations are proposed: (A) allelopathic recruitment suppression, (B) herbivore functional group collapse. What single additional observation most efficiently distinguishes between these two causes, and why?
Un estudio GRIN registra 18% de cobertura de macroalgas y densidad de juveniles casi nula. Se proponen dos interpretaciones: (A) supresión de reclutamiento alelopática, (B) colapso del grupo funcional de herbívoros. ¿Cuál observación adicional distingue más eficientemente entre estas dos causas?
  • A. Water chemistry — allelopathy elevates dissolved organic carbon detectable by MOOP sensors, while herbivore collapse does not alter water chemistry
  • B. Repeat survey in 90 days — if algae cover increases, herbivore collapse is the cause; if algae cover is stable, allelopathy is the cause
  • C. Recruitment density in the 20 cm buffer zone adjacent to algae patches versus open substrate away from algae: if recruits are absent only near algae patches but present on open substrate away from them, allelopathy is the primary mechanism; if recruits are absent from both zones equally, herbivore functional group collapse is the primary mechanism — because allelopathy suppresses settlement locally while herbivore absence suppresses the post-settlement survival and substrate preparation globally
  • D. Coral cover percentage — if coral cover exceeds 25%, allelopathy is the cause; if below 25%, herbivore collapse is the cause
ANSWER: C · RESPUESTA: C
QUESTION 02
Shark and large grouper encounter rates at a corridor site decline 70% between circuits 3 and 4. Herbivore biomass and algae cover are unchanged. Why should the CMIE enter a trophic cascade flag now rather than waiting for a measurable benthic signal, and what intervention window does early entry create?
Las tasas de encuentro con tiburones y mero grande disminuyen un 70% entre los circuitos 3 y 4. La biomasa de herbívoros y la cobertura de algas no han cambiado. ¿Por qué el CMIE debe registrar una señal de cascada ahora y qué ventana de intervención crea?
  • A. The CMIE should not enter a cascade flag without measurable herbivore or algae change — apex predator decline alone is insufficient evidence of cascade initiation given natural variability in encounter rates
  • B. The flag should be entered as a precaution only, with low confidence weighting, and upgraded to confirmed cascade when herbivore data changes
  • C. The cascade operates with sequential temporal lags — apex removal precedes mesopredator release by months to years, which precedes herbivore suppression, which precedes algae bloom; entering the flag at the apex level rather than waiting for the benthic signal buys the full cascade lag time as a management window; a 70% apex decline is the highest-leverage intervention point in the cascade — fisheries protection or no-take zone establishment at this stage can halt the cascade before it becomes self-reinforcing at the algae-dominated end-state
  • D. The flag should be entered in GKIN as a pelagic predator anomaly rather than in the CMIE, since apex predators are primarily nekton and belong in the kinetic index record
ANSWER: C · RESPUESTA: C
QUESTION 03
A corridor site has active SCTLD confirmed across 8 species and a concurrent DHW-4 thermal bleaching event. A junior analyst proposes summing the projected SCTLD mortality rate and the projected bleaching mortality rate to estimate total coral loss. What is wrong with this approach and what does GROAN's CMIE do instead?
Un sitio tiene SCTLD activa confirmada en 8 especies y un evento de blanqueamiento térmico DHW-4 concurrente. Un analista propone sumar las tasas de mortalidad proyectadas de SCTLD y blanqueamiento para estimar la pérdida total de coral. ¿Qué está mal con este enfoque y qué hace el CMIE de GROAN en su lugar?
  • A. Nothing is wrong — summing independent stressor projections is the standard approach when two stressors affect different species; the CMIE uses the same method with higher taxonomic resolution
  • B. The approach overestimates mortality because corals cannot die twice — shared affected species must be counted once; the CMIE applies a species overlap correction factor
  • C. The linear sum underestimates combined mortality because SCTLD and thermal stress interact non-additively — thermal stress degrades coral immune function and tissue integrity, increasing susceptibility to SCTLD progression; bleaching-stressed corals show accelerated SCTLD tissue loss rates compared to unstressed corals; the CMIE enters SCTLD and bleaching as separate signals, then applies a compound stressor interaction weighting that reflects the documented synergistic mortality amplification, producing a combined DS that exceeds the linear sum of the two individual stressor scores
  • D. The approach is correct for SCTLD and bleaching specifically because they operate through independent pathways (pathogen vs. thermal), but would be wrong for two thermal stressors acting on the same mechanism
ANSWER: C · RESPUESTA: C
