Comparative Systems

MAR · GBR · Red Sea · Coral Triangle · E. Pacific — chemical architecture, transferable lessons, and CMIE cross-system evidence rules

Lecture
EN — English

Comparative systems analysis is not an academic exercise in GROAN. It is a calibration discipline. CMIE draws on chemical evidence from multiple reef systems to generate cross-ecosystem attributions — which means every lesson from the GBR's bleaching record, the Red Sea's thermal chemistry, or the Coral Triangle's throughflow dynamics carries operational weight in a Caribbean DS calculation. The rule that governs all cross-system evidence transfer is explicit: transferable lessons travel through mechanism, not geography. A GBR bleaching response function is applicable to a MAR reef zone only if the carbonate chemistry, thermal stress trajectory, and biological community structure are sufficiently analogous. Applying GBR thermal thresholds to the Eastern Pacific upwelling zone without endmember correction is not conservative — it is wrong.

System 01 · Mesoamerican Reef (MAR) — GROAN Primary Theater
Ω 2.8–3.8 Karst-dominated 7-country arc Rim Run™ ground-truth

The MAR is GROAN's primary chemical reference system — the theater with the densest in-situ MOOP coverage, the most complete annual Rim Run™ corridor record, and the most complex multi-stressor attribution challenge. Its defining chemical character is the karst geology–terrestrial connectivity coupling: karst SGD, agricultural DIN, and seasonal freshwater dilution operate simultaneously against a Caribbean carbonate baseline that is inherently higher-Ω than global average. The MAR is the system against which all cross-system evidence transfers are evaluated for applicability — not because it is typical, but because it is the ground-truth anchor of the entire GROAN corridor. Lessons from other systems apply to the MAR when the controlling stressor mechanism is chemically analogous, not when the reef type or geography matches.

System 02 · Great Barrier Reef (GBR) — Bleaching Reference
Ω 3.2–3.8 Thermal benchmark Strongest dataset DHW validated

The GBR is the world's most extensively monitored coral reef system — its thermal bleaching record, DHW response functions, and carbonate chemistry datasets form the empirical foundation of most global reef bleaching models. For GROAN, the GBR provides the highest-quality thermal stress × carbonate chemistry interaction data: the relationship between SST anomaly, Ω, and bleaching probability is better characterized at the GBR than anywhere else. Transferable to MAR via the thermal stress × OA synergistic pathway — DHW thresholds and bleaching probability curves are applicable when GBR ambient Ω (3.2–3.8) is comparable to the target MAR zone. When MAR Ω is suppressed by karst SGD below 3.0, the GBR bleaching curves must be shifted — lower Ω amplifies bleaching response at lower DHW. Non-transferable: GBR's offshore, low-terrestrial-input chemistry is not applicable to MAR lagoonal or near-shore stations dominated by SGD and agricultural runoff.

System 03 · Red Sea — Thermal-Chemical Amplification Reference
Ω 3.5–4.2 Hypersaline data Seagrass buffer

The Red Sea is GROAN's proof-of-concept case for thermal-chemical amplification across ecosystem boundaries — the seagrass buffering collapse → reef bleaching amplification causal chain. Its exceptionally high TA (~2450 µmol/kg) means it operates at elevated carbonate buffering capacity relative to any Caribbean system, making direct Ω comparison invalid. The Red Sea's transferable lesson for GROAN is mechanistic, not numerical: the seagrass metabolic suppression signal (diel DO amplitude collapse below 2 mg/L) as an early warning of thermal stress amplification is applicable wherever seagrass meadows buffer adjacent reef zones — including Costa Rica's Caribbean coast and Belize's southern lagoon system. The specific Ω thresholds are non-transferable; the biological chemical mechanism is fully transferable.

System 04 · Coral Triangle — Multi-Stressor Integration Reference
Ω 3.2–3.9 Throughflow mixing Max. biodiversity CTI partnership

The Coral Triangle is the world's most chemically complex reef system — Indonesian Throughflow water mass mixing, intense artisanal and industrial fishing pressure, Crown-of-Thorns outbreaks, and coastal development stressors operate simultaneously across six national jurisdictions. Its chemical lesson for GROAN is multi-stressor attribution under data-sparse conditions: the CTI has developed multi-stressor frameworks — integrating thermal, chemical, biological, and human pressure signals — that CMIE can apply where the MAR data record is thin. Transferable: the principle that single-stressor DS calculations systematically underestimate combined stress in high-biodiversity, high-pressure systems. The specific endmember chemistry is non-transferable — the Throughflow signature has no analogue in the Caribbean.

System 05 · Eastern Pacific — Upwelling OA Reference
Ω 1.5–3.2 Natural low-Ω ENSO-governed Panama corridor terminus

The Eastern Pacific upwelling zone provides GROAN's reference case for naturally low-Ω reef chemistry — the critical baseline for understanding what carbonate suppression looks like when it is natural and periodic rather than anthropogenic and secular. The Panama Bight and Costa Rica Dome have the lowest naturally occurring Ω in any GROAN theater. Their chemical lesson: coral communities can acclimatize to periodic low-Ω conditions when OA is natural and cyclical, but the same communities show catastrophic bleaching and dissolution when OA-acclimated reefs experience superimposed anthropogenic acidification on top of upwelling suppression. This is the mechanistic basis for CMIE's mandatory upwelling index correction at Panama corridor stations — distinguishing natural periodic suppression from the secular trend matters because the biological responses are fundamentally different.

Key argument: GROAN's CMIE is not a MAR-specific model — it is a cross-system evidence integration engine. Its ability to draw on the GBR bleaching record, Red Sea seagrass chemistry, Coral Triangle multi-stressor frameworks, and Eastern Pacific upwelling baselines to improve MAR Decision Scores is the structural advantage that distinguishes it from every single-system monitoring program operating in the Caribbean today. No regional monitoring body has this evidence architecture. Each operates within its own system's data. CMIE operates across all of them simultaneously — constrained by the transferability rules that prevent false analogy while exploiting every mechanistically valid cross-system lesson.

ES — Español

El análisis de sistemas comparativos no es un ejercicio académico en GROAN. Es una disciplina de calibración. La regla que rige toda la transferencia de evidencia entre sistemas es explícita: las lecciones transferibles viajan a través del mecanismo, no de la geografía. Una función de respuesta al blanqueamiento del GBR es aplicable a una zona arrecifal del MAR solo si la química de carbonatos, la trayectoria del estrés térmico y la estructura de la comunidad biológica son suficientemente análogas.

MAR: El teatro de referencia primario de GROAN — la única fuente de verdad terreno del corredor. Las lecciones de otros sistemas se aplican al MAR cuando el mecanismo de control del estresor es químicamente análogo, no cuando el tipo de arrecife o la geografía coinciden.

GBR: Proporciona los datos de interacción más de alta calidad entre estrés térmico y química de carbonatos. Transferible al MAR a través de la vía sinérgica estrés térmico × OA cuando el Ω ambiente del GBR (3.2–3.8) es comparable a la zona MAR objetivo. No transferible: la química del GBR fuera de la costa sin aportes terrestres no es aplicable a estaciones lagunares del MAR dominadas por SGD.

Mar Rojo: La lección transferible es mecanística, no numérica: la señal de supresión metabólica de los pastos marinos (colapso de la amplitud diurna de OD por debajo de 2 mg/L) es aplicable donde los pastos marinos amortiguan zonas arrecifales adyacentes — incluyendo la costa caribeña de Costa Rica y el sistema lagunar del sur de Belice.

Triángulo de Coral: La lección es que los cálculos de DS de estresor único subestiman sistemáticamente el estrés combinado en sistemas de alta biodiversidad y alta presión. La química de endmember específica no es transferible.

Pacífico Oriental: Las comunidades de coral pueden aclimatarse a condiciones periódicas de Ω bajo cuando la OA es natural y cíclica, pero las mismas comunidades muestran blanqueamiento catastrófico cuando la acidificación antropogénica se superpone a la supresión por surgencia. Esta es la base mecanística para la corrección obligatoria del índice de surgencia de CMIE en las estaciones del corredor de Panamá.

Argumento clave: El CMIE de GROAN no es un modelo específico del MAR — es un motor de integración de evidencia entre sistemas. Su capacidad para extraer del registro de blanqueamiento del GBR, la química de pastos marinos del Mar Rojo, los marcos multi-estresores del Triángulo de Coral y las líneas base de surgencia del Pacífico Oriental para mejorar los Puntajes de Decisión del MAR es la ventaja estructural que lo distingue de cada programa de monitoreo de sistema único que opera en el Caribe hoy.

Application
EN — English
  • 01When applying GBR bleaching probability curves to a MAR reef zone CMIE calculation, first confirm that the target zone's ambient Ω is within the GBR comparable range of 3.2–3.8. If MAR Ω is suppressed below 3.0 by karst SGD or agricultural loading, the GBR bleaching curves must be shifted — lower Ω amplifies bleaching response at lower DHW values than the GBR baseline predicts. Using uncorrected GBR curves at a suppressed-Ω MAR station will systematically underestimate bleaching probability at every DHW level.
  • 02When CMIE detects a seagrass diel DO amplitude collapse below 2 mg/L at any MAR station adjacent to a seagrass meadow — particularly in Costa Rica's southern Caribbean or Belize's southern lagoon — apply the Red Sea thermal-chemical amplification mechanism. The seagrass buffering collapse signal is mechanistically identical in both systems even though the absolute Ω and TA values differ. The mechanism transfers; the endmember numbers do not. Route the DS elevation using the Red Sea-validated mechanism but calibrated against MAR regional chemistry.
  • 03When a MAR corridor site has sparse chemical data — particularly in Nicaragua where data gaps are documented — apply the Coral Triangle multi-stressor framework as a conservative DS floor rather than generating a low-confidence single-stressor estimate. The CT framework's principle that combined stressors in data-sparse, high-pressure systems are systematically underestimated by single-stressor models is transferable as a methodological posture, even when the specific CT chemistry is not applicable. Document the framework application in the CMIE calibration record with the transferability condition stated explicitly.
  • 04Never apply any regional TA endmember value from a non-Caribbean system to a MAR MOOP station. This restriction applies without exception to GBR (~2300 µmol/kg open ocean), Red Sea (~2450 µmol/kg hypersaline), Coral Triangle (~2280 µmol/kg Pacific-influenced), and Eastern Pacific (~2250 µmol/kg upwelling). The Caribbean MAR baseline (~2300 µmol/kg wet season, ~2380 µmol/kg dry season with mangrove buffering) must be used exclusively for MAR carbonate calculations. Regional endmember values are the one category of cross-system data that is never transferable — see L20 for the quantified consequences of this error.
  • 05When generating a CMIE DS for a Panama corridor station that uses Eastern Pacific upwelling correction, explicitly document in the calibration record that this station cannot be compared directly to any other MAR corridor station without the upwelling correction applied. Panama stations post-correction are comparable to other MAR stations on the anomaly scale — but their raw chemistry is not comparable to Mexico, Belize, or Honduras stations. A future researcher pooling uncorrected Panama data with the rest of the corridor record will generate a systematic artifact in any longitudinal trend analysis. The documentation prevents this downstream error.
