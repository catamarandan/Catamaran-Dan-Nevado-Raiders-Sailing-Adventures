Field Methods

Knowing which instruments to use is necessary but not sufficient. How you deploy them — station protocol, sample handling, QC checks, and metadata discipline — determines whether your data earns a High confidence tier or gets discarded by the CMIE.

Lecture
EN

Field methods are the operational translation of measurement theory (L08) into executable station protocol. A scientist who understands carbonate chemistry but deploys sensors incorrectly produces data that looks valid but carries systematic errors invisible to the CMIE. GROAN's confidence tier system is only as honest as the field methods that generate the underlying readings. This lesson covers the MOOP station protocol in full — from pre-departure checks to post-processing submission — as it will be executed aboard Shamrocket during the Rim Run™ circuit.

PRE
Pre-Departure Calibration
pH sensor: 3-point calibration using NIST-traceable buffers (pH 4.0, 7.0, 10.0) — log calibration slope and offset before every transit leg. DO sensor: Air-saturated water or sodium sulfite zero check. CTD: Conductivity verified against certified saline standard. Titration kit: Verify HCl normality against certified CaCO₃ standard. Any sensor failing calibration is tagged LOW confidence for that leg — data collected but flagged.
ARR
Station Arrival Protocol
Log GPS coordinates, local time, and tidal phase before any sensor deployment. Tidal phase is determined from regional tidal model (TPXO or equivalent) at station coordinates — not estimated. Record sky conditions, sea state, and visible disturbance (active runoff, algal bloom, boat wake turbulence). Note depth at station. All metadata recorded in standardized MOOP station log — handwritten and digital backup.
DEP
Sensor Deployment Sequence
Step 1 — CTD cast: Lower sensor package slowly to target depth (reef crest: 2–5 m; lagoon: 1–3 m; mangrove fringe: surface). Hold 90 seconds for equilibration before logging. Step 2 — Niskin bottle: Collect 250 mL water sample at same depth as CTD cast. Overflow bottle 3× before capping — eliminates headspace that alters TA. Add 100 µL saturated HgCl₂ immediately to poison sample (halts biological activity). Step 3 — DO reading: Record at equilibration depth. Note if sensor reads above 110% saturation (supersaturation = active photosynthesis; flags diel timing requirement).
QC
On-Site Quality Control
Duplicate cast: Every 5th station, conduct a duplicate CTD cast and collect a second Niskin sample. Duplicate pH readings should agree within ±0.02; if discrepancy exceeds ±0.05, station is flagged for resampling. Sensor drift check: Re-verify pH sensor against field reference buffer after cast — if drift exceeds ±0.02 from pre-departure calibration, all readings from that leg are down-tiered to Moderate. Temperature cross-check: CTD temperature vs. handheld thermometer — discrepancy >0.2°C triggers sensor inspection.
POST
Sample Handling & Post-Processing
TA samples: Store at 4°C, dark. Titrate within 72 hours of collection. Post-titration, run CO2SYS with pH + TA to calculate Ω, DIC, pCO₂. Data submission: All field data enters GROAN API Registry (L1) within 24 hours of station completion. Confidence tier is computed at edge node before upload — Low-confidence packets compressed to minimum resolution; High-confidence packets transmitted at full resolution. Station log signed and archived.

Field method errors that invalidate data. The most common field method failures are procedural, not instrumental. Four errors account for the majority of data quality failures in Caribbean reef chemistry campaigns:

Error 01 — Headspace Contamination

Collecting a Niskin sample with air headspace allows CO₂ gas exchange that alters TA by 5–15 µmol/kg before titration. Result: false low-TA reading → inflated OA alarm.

Fix 01 — Overflow Protocol

Overflow bottle 3× before capping. Add HgCl₂ immediately. Store inverted. A correctly collected sample is stable for 72 hours and produces TA accurate to ±3 µmol/kg in field conditions.

Error 02 — Sensor Equilibration Skip

Logging pH immediately after immersion before thermal equilibration of the ISFET element. A 1°C disequilibrium introduces ~0.01–0.02 pH unit error. Result: confident-looking data with systematic offset.

Fix 02 — 90-Second Rule

Hold sensor at target depth for minimum 90 seconds before logging. Watch for stable reading on display — pH drift <0.003 units over 30 seconds signals equilibration. Document wait time in station log.

Error 03 — Missing Tidal Metadata

Recording only clock time without tidal phase. As established in L05, the same station can swing 0.3 pH units between flood and ebb. Result: data with correct values but uninterpretable context — automatic Low confidence tier.

Fix 03 — Tidal Phase Stamp

Use TPXO tidal model output pre-loaded for all 100+ Rim Run™ station coordinates. At arrival, match GPS timestamp to tidal phase table. Record phase as flood/ebb/slack with hours from nearest high water.

Error 04 — Single-Depth Sampling

Collecting all samples at one depth when the reef zone has a shallow thermocline or chemocline. In mangrove fringe sites, DO and pH can differ by 2 mg/L and 0.4 units between surface and 2 m depth. Result: unrepresentative snapshot assigned full station weight.

Fix 04 — Depth Profile Rule

At all mangrove fringe (GMIN) and lagoon (GMAIN) stations, conduct minimum 3-depth CTD profile: surface (0.5 m), mid-water (half depth), and bottom (−0.5 m from substrate). Flag chemocline presence in station log for CMIE stratification input.

ES

Los métodos de campo son la traducción operacional de la teoría de medición (L08) en un protocolo de estación ejecutable. El sistema de niveles de confianza de GROAN es tan honesto como los métodos de campo que generan las lecturas subyacentes. El protocolo de estación MOOP cubre cinco fases: calibración previa a la salida, protocolo de llegada a la estación, secuencia de despliegue de sensores, control de calidad en sitio, y manejo de muestras y post-procesamiento.

La calibración previa a la salida requiere calibración de 3 puntos del sensor de pH con tampones trazables a NIST, verificación de DO, verificación de conductividad CTD y normalidad del kit de titulación. Cualquier sensor que falle la calibración se etiqueta como BAJA confianza para esa etapa. El protocolo de llegada exige registrar coordenadas GPS, hora local y fase de marea determinada del modelo de mareas regional TPXO antes de cualquier despliegue de sensor. La secuencia de despliegue sigue: sonda CTD (90 segundos de equilibración), botella Niskin (desbordar 3× antes de tapar, añadir HgCl₂ inmediatamente), lectura de DO.

Los cuatro errores de campo más comunes que invalidan datos: (1) contaminación por espacio de cabeza en muestras Niskin — corregida con el protocolo de desbordamiento; (2) omisión de equilibración del sensor — corregida con la regla de 90 segundos; (3) falta de metadatos de fase de marea — corregida con el sello de fase de TPXO; (4) muestreo a una sola profundidad en sitios estratificados — corregida con perfil CTD mínimo de 3 profundidades en estaciones GMIN y GMAIN.

Application
EN
  • The Rim Run™ circuit covers 100+ stations across 7 countries in a single October–November transit. Every station must produce GRIN-credible data on the first pass — there is no return visit. The MOOP protocol's pre-departure calibration and on-site QC checks are not optional quality enhancements; they are the only mechanism preventing a failed sensor from silently generating a false baseline across an entire national reef system.
  • The 90-second equilibration rule has a practical Shamrocket implication: the catamaran should be anchored or drifting with minimal wake before sensor deployment. Active motoring creates turbulence that introduces surface water into the sample depth, degrading pH and DO readings — the protocol note is "engines off before sensor in water."
  • HgCl₂ poisoning of Niskin samples is chemically essential but operationally hazardous — MOOP's field protocol must include proper hazmat handling procedures (gloves, sealed waste container, no overboard disposal) at every station. Port authorities in 3 of the 7 Rim Run™ countries may require declaration of HgCl₂ as a hazardous material aboard Shamrocket.
  • The 4-error / 4-fix framework maps directly to the GSIN Technical Specification's edge compression logic: field errors that produce Low-confidence data should be flagged at the edge node before upload, not discovered in post-processing. Implementing real-time QC alerts on the MOOP sensor display — drift alarm, equilibration timer, tidal-phase reminder — converts the protocol from a checklist into an automated quality gate.
  • For KAUST Red Sea protocol adaptation: the mangrove fringe 3-depth profile rule applies with modification — Red Sea reef sites have distinct thermocline and halocline structures at 10–20 m depth rather than 1–3 m. GSIN station protocols for the Red Sea case study should specify minimum 5-depth profiles at stratified stations.
ES
  • El circuito Rim Run™ cubre más de 100 estaciones en 7 países en un solo tránsito de octubre a noviembre. Cada estación debe producir datos creíbles para GRIN en el primer paso — no hay visita de regreso. Las verificaciones de calibración y QC en sitio del protocolo MOOP son el único mecanismo que previene que un sensor defectuoso genere silenciosamente una línea de base falsa en todo un sistema arrecifal nacional.
  • La regla de equilibración de 90 segundos tiene una implicación práctica para Shamrocket: el catamarán debe estar anclado o derivando con mínima estela antes del despliegue del sensor. La navegación activa crea turbulencia que introduce agua superficial en la profundidad de muestreo, degradando las lecturas de pH y DO — la nota de protocolo es "motores apagados antes de sumergir el sensor."
  • El envenenamiento con HgCl₂ de las muestras Niskin es químicamente esencial pero operativamente peligroso — el protocolo de campo MOOP debe incluir procedimientos adecuados de manejo de materiales peligrosos (guantes, recipiente de residuos sellado, sin disposición por la borda) en cada estación. Las autoridades portuarias en 3 de los 7 países Rim Run™ pueden requerir declaración del HgCl₂ como material peligroso a bordo de Shamrocket.
  • El marco de 4 errores / 4 soluciones se mapea directamente a la lógica de compresión de borde de la Especificación Técnica GSIN: los errores de campo que producen datos de Baja Confianza deben marcarse en el nodo de borde antes de la carga, no descubrirse en el post-procesamiento. Implementar alertas de QC en tiempo real en la pantalla del sensor MOOP convierte el protocolo de una lista de verificación en una puerta de calidad automatizada.
  • Para la adaptación del protocolo del Mar Rojo para KAUST: la regla de perfil de 3 profundidades del franja de manglar aplica con modificación — los sitios arrecifales del Mar Rojo tienen termoclina y haloclina distintas a 10–20 m de profundidad en lugar de 1–3 m. Los protocolos de estación GSIN para el estudio de caso del Mar Rojo deben especificar perfiles mínimos de 5 profundidades en estaciones estratificadas.
