Stressors

Chemical stressor taxonomy, interaction architecture, and CMIE multiplicative weighting

Lecture
EN — English

A stressor is not an indicator reading. An indicator tells CMIE where a system is. A stressor tells CMIE why it got there and — critically — which other stressors are amplifying or moderating the response. GROAN defines six primary chemical stressors. The single most important architectural fact about these stressors is that their interactions are not additive — they are multiplicative or synergistic. A reef experiencing two stressors simultaneously is not twice as stressed as a reef experiencing one. It may be four times as stressed, or it may be approaching a tipping point where recovery becomes structurally impossible. CMIE's cross-module interaction weighting exists to capture this non-linearity. No single-ecosystem monitoring framework can compute it.

Stressor 01 · Ocean Acidification
pH −0.002/yr Ω secular ↓ Long-term trend

The global long-term background stressor — atmospheric CO₂ uptake driving a secular ocean pH decline of ~0.002 units per year and systematic Ω suppression. OA operates on decadal timescales; it is the baseline against which all episodic acidification events are measured. CMIE must separate OA trend signal from episodic stressors: a single low-Ω event is not OA evidence. OA is confirmed by a declining multi-year Ω trend at corridor sites combined with Argo or CORDAP open-ocean records. Primary GROAN module: GRIN (calcification). Synergistic with thermal stress — the canonical OA × Thermal interaction is the Red Sea proof-of-concept case.

Stressor 02 · Thermal Stress
DHW >4 → bleach DHW >8 → severe MMM + 1°C trigger

Elevated SST quantified by Degree Heating Weeks (DHW) — the accumulated thermal anomaly above the maximum monthly mean. DHW >4 predicts bleaching; DHW >8 predicts severe bleaching and mortality. Chemically, thermal stress drives a measurable pCO₂ rise (warmer water holds less CO₂) and suppresses coral calcification, reducing TA drawdown. In the Red Sea, SST anomaly ≥1°C MMM for ≥4 weeks triggers GSIN seagrass metabolic stress before reef bleaching appears. Thermal stress is GROAN's most synergistically dangerous stressor — its OA interaction and its eutrophication interaction are both classified as synergistic in the CMIE interaction matrix.

Stressor 03 · Eutrophication
DIN >3 µmol/L N:P >16 Phase shift risk

Excess nutrient loading from agricultural runoff, sewage, and aquaculture driving algal proliferation, increased respiration, DO depletion, and local CO₂ production. Chemically: elevated DIN, N:P >16 (agricultural signature), DO suppression at night, pCO₂ elevation during algal decomposition. Eutrophication is the primary phase-shift accelerant — elevated nutrients shift the competitive advantage from coral to macroalgae, and the chemical signature (elevated respiration, DO suppression) precedes the visible phase shift by weeks. Synergistic with both thermal stress (warm water accelerates bacterial decomposition) and OA (local CO₂ production from respiration further depresses Ω).

Stressor 04 · Freshwater Discharge
TA dilution Ω suppression Wet season / flood

River flooding and storm surge deliver large volumes of low-alkalinity, low-pH freshwater directly to reef zones — particularly acute along the MAR corridor in Guatemala and Honduras during wet season and post-hurricane events. The chemical mechanism: freshwater dilutes TA, suppressing Ω even when absolute pH may not change dramatically. In flood events, turbidity simultaneously blocks light, compounding the chemical stress with a physical stressor. Freshwater discharge is additive with eutrophication (runoff carries both low-TA water and DIN simultaneously) and additive with SGD in karst environments. Post-hurricane freshwater surges along the MAR have historically driven Ω below 2.0 at reef flat stations within 48 hours.

Stressor 05 · Organic Loading
DO ↓ (BOD) pCO₂ ↑ H₂S threshold

Decomposing organic matter — primarily sargassum influx events, but also terrestrial DOC loading and aquaculture waste — exerts biological oxygen demand (BOD) that suppresses DO and elevates pCO₂ as decomposition releases CO₂. In extreme sargassum events, anaerobic decomposition produces hydrogen sulfide (H₂S), a toxin with a zero-tolerance threshold — any H₂S detection at a reef zone station triggers an immediate Critical alert in CMIE, regardless of other indicator levels. The H₂S threshold is not a monitoring escalation — it is an acute toxicity event requiring immediate DS elevation.

Stressor 06 · SGD Chemical Stress
pH ↓ (soil CO₂) DO ↓ Structural (MAR)

Submarine groundwater discharge delivers low-pH, low-DO, high-DIN water that has equilibrated with soil CO₂ in the aquifer. In the MAR, karst SGD is structural and continuous — a permanent background stressor that must be corrected before any other stressor is attributed. Outside the MAR, SGD is episodic, triggered by rainfall recharge events and tidal pumping. SGD interacts additively with both OA (further pH suppression) and freshwater discharge (both dilute TA). The critical CMIE distinction: SGD-sourced DIN is isotopically distinct from agricultural DIN (δ¹⁵N ~2–5‰ vs ~8–12‰) — source attribution prevents CMIE from misidentifying the intervention target.

The interaction matrix. CMIE's weighting architecture uses three interaction classifications — Synergistic (combined effect > sum of parts), Additive (combined effect ≈ sum of parts), and Independent (stressors do not amplify each other):

Key argument: The three synergistic pairs — OA × Thermal, OA × Eutrophication, Thermal × Eutrophication — are the mechanistic core of CMIE's PhD contribution. A monitoring system that tracks these stressors independently will underestimate combined reef stress by a factor of 1.5–3× compared to the CMIE interaction-weighted DS. That underestimate is not a rounding error — it is the difference between a management decision to maintain existing protocols versus a decision to mobilize immediate intervention.

ES — Español

Un estresor no es una lectura de indicador. Un indicador le dice a CMIE dónde está un sistema. Un estresor le dice a CMIE por qué llegó ahí y — de manera crítica — qué otros estresores están amplificando o moderando la respuesta. GROAN define seis estresores químicos primarios. El hecho arquitectónico más importante es que sus interacciones no son aditivas — son multiplicativas o sinérgicas. CMIE existe para capturar esta no-linealidad.

Acidificación Oceánica (OA): El estresor de fondo global a largo plazo — descenso secular del pH oceánico de ~0.002 unidades por año. CMIE debe separar la señal de tendencia OA de los estresores episódicos: un único evento de Ω bajo no es evidencia de OA. Sinérgico con el estrés térmico — el caso del Mar Rojo.

Estrés Térmico: Cuantificado por Semanas de Calentamiento (DHW). DHW >4 predice blanqueamiento; DHW >8 predice blanqueamiento severo. El estrés térmico es el estresor sinérgicamente más peligroso de GROAN — sus interacciones con OA y eutrofización se clasifican ambas como sinérgicas.

Eutrofización: El principal acelerador de cambio de fase coral→algas. Sinérgico tanto con el estrés térmico (el agua cálida acelera la descomposición bacteriana) como con OA (la producción local de CO₂ por respiración deprime aún más Ω).

Descarga de Agua Dulce: Diluye la AT, suprimiendo Ω incluso cuando el pH absoluto no cambia dramáticamente. Aditivo con eutrofización (la escorrentía transporta agua de baja AT y DIN simultáneamente).

Carga Orgánica: Materia orgánica en descomposición ejerce demanda biológica de oxígeno suprimiendo OD y elevando pCO₂. La producción de H₂S en eventos extremos tiene umbral cero tolerancia — cualquier detección de H₂S activa una alerta Crítica inmediata CMIE.

SGD Químico: Descarga de aguas subterráneas de bajo pH, bajo OD, alto DIN. Estructural en el MAR; episódico en otros lugares. La atribución de fuente por δ¹⁵N evita que CMIE identifique erróneamente el objetivo de intervención.

Argumento clave: Los tres pares sinérgicos — OA × Térmico, OA × Eutrofización, Térmico × Eutrofización — son el núcleo mecánico de la contribución doctoral del CMIE. Un sistema de monitoreo que rastrea estos estresores de forma independiente subestimará el estrés combinado del arrecife en un factor de 1.5–3× en comparación con el DS ponderado por interacción del CMIE.

Application
EN — English
  • 01Never classify a single low-Ω or low-pH event as Ocean Acidification signal in CMIE. OA is confirmed only by a declining multi-year trend at tidal phase-matched corridor sites combined with an open-ocean record from Argo floats or CORDAP. A single event may be episodic acidification from freshwater discharge, SGD, sargassum decomposition, or upwelling — each requiring a different intervention target. Misattributing episodic stress to OA directs resources to the wrong timescale and policy lever.
  • 02When DHW exceeds 4 at a reef zone, automatically apply the OA × Thermal synergistic weight in CMIE — not the thermal DS alone. The synergistic weight reflects that OA-suppressed Ω magnifies the bleaching response to thermal stress. A reef already at Ω 2.8 will bleach at lower DHW than a reef at Ω 3.5. Applying the standalone thermal DS without the OA interaction context systematically underestimates bleaching probability at all MAR corridor sites where Ω has been declining.
  • 03During wet season flood events in Guatemala and Honduras, treat co-occurring freshwater TA dilution and elevated DIN from agricultural runoff as a single compound stressor — not two independent inputs. Both arrive simultaneously via the same river discharge mechanism, and their combined Ω suppression plus nutrient loading effect is additive in CMIE. Processing them independently will produce two separate lower-priority DS signals when the correct output is one elevated compound DS.
  • 04If H₂S is detected at any MOOP station — by smell, by sensor, or by instrument anomaly consistent with sulfide interference — immediately elevate the reef zone DS to Critical regardless of all other indicator levels. H₂S is an acute reef toxin with no threshold below which it is acceptable. Do not downgrade to Alert pending confirmation from a second station. A single H₂S detection is sufficient for the Critical classification. Document the event timestamp, GPS position, and instrument reading in the CMIE calibration record before leaving the site.
  • 05When a reef zone under agricultural or tourism pressure has no targeted organic chemistry data (pesticides, herbicides, oxybenzone, octinoxate), document the data gap explicitly in the CMIE calibration record before generating a DS for that zone. Absence of pollution data is not evidence of absence of pollution. The DS generated without this data carries an undocumented uncertainty that may materially understate intervention urgency. Flag the gap, note which compound classes are unsampled, and record it as a data collection priority for the next Rim Run™ circuit.
