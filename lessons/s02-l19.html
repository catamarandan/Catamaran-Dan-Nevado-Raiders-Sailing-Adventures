Case Studies

Caribbean carbonate disruption & Red Sea thermal-chemical amplification

Lecture
EN — English

GROAN's two primary proof-of-concept case studies are not selected arbitrarily. They are chosen because each demonstrates a specific structural argument: that cross-ecosystem chemical signals, processed through the Cross-Module Interaction Engine, produce decisions that single-ecosystem monitoring frameworks are structurally incapable of generating. The Caribbean case proves CMIE's capacity for terrestrial-to-reef causal attribution. The Red Sea case proves CMIE's capacity for thermal-chemical amplification detection across ecosystem boundaries. Neither conclusion is available without cross-module signal integration.

Case Study 01 · Caribbean · CORDAP Data
GMIN · Mangrove Loss → TA ↓ · Ω ↓ · DIN ↑ → GMAIN · Juvenile Fish ↓ → GRIN · Trophic Disruption → CMIE · DS Elevated

Mangrove forests in the Caribbean serve as critical alkalinity sources. Root respiration and organic matter decomposition export bicarbonate-rich, high-alkalinity water into adjacent reef zones, buffering the local carbonate system against daytime metabolic acidification. When mangrove coverage contracts — through coastal development, deforestation, or storm damage — this alkalinity subsidy is removed. The chemical consequence at the downstream reef zone is measurable: total alkalinity (TA) decreases 30–80 µmol/kg in the wet season discharge plume, suppressing Ωarag from above 3.0 to the 1.8–2.2 danger zone. Simultaneously, reduced mangrove filtration capacity allows elevated dissolved inorganic nitrogen (DIN) to reach the reef, promoting macroalgae growth and intensifying the trophic signal that GRIN detects.

A single-ecosystem monitoring framework watching only the reef would observe suppressed Ω and elevated nutrients — and could attribute this to ocean acidification trend or local runoff. Only CMIE, receiving concurrent GMIN coverage-loss data, GMAIN juvenile fish decline metrics, and GRIN carbonate suppression signals, can generate the correct causal attribution: mangrove loss is the primary driver. The Decision Score outcome — intervention at the mangrove watershed level, not at the reef — is structurally unavailable without cross-module integration. CORDAP data provides the reef carbonate baseline and temporal coverage for this case in the Caribbean theater.

Case Study 02 · Red Sea · KAUST Data
SST Anomaly >1°C → DO Amp ↓ · pCO₂ ↑ · pH ↓ → GSIN · Seagrass Stress → GRIN · Bleaching Amplified → CMIE · DS Critical

The Red Sea case demonstrates a thermal-chemical amplification pathway. Seagrass meadows in the Red Sea perform a carbonate buffering function analogous to mangroves in the Caribbean — net photosynthesis during daylight hours drives CO₂ uptake, locally elevating pH and Ωarag in adjacent reef zones. The chemical signature of a healthy seagrass meadow is a diel DO amplitude >3 mg/L and a suppressed daytime pCO₂ signal. When SST exceeds 1°C above the maximum monthly mean (MMM) for four or more consecutive weeks, seagrass metabolic activity decelerates. The chemical consequence is diagnostic: DO diel amplitude compresses below 2 mg/L, pCO₂ rises, and daytime Ω buffering disappears.

Adjacent reef corals, which depend on this seagrass-mediated buffering during thermal stress events, now experience both elevated temperature and chemical destabilization simultaneously. The bleaching risk is not the sum of thermal stress plus acidification stress — it is a multiplicative amplification. CMIE receives the Feature Layer SST anomaly, the GSIN seagrass chemical stress flag, and the GRIN bleaching probability signal, and attributes the elevated DS to the cross-ecosystem amplification mechanism — not to thermal forcing alone. KAUST Red Sea Research Center data provides the SST anomaly record and seagrass chemical baseline for this case. The Red Sea endmember pCO₂ and alkalinity values differ materially from Caribbean defaults and must not be substituted with generic ocean parameters.

Key argument: Both case studies produce the same structural proof. The chemical parameters — TA, Ω, DIN, DO amplitude, pCO₂ — are not the contribution. The contribution is the causal chain CMIE constructs across ecosystem boundaries using those parameters. Remove any single module from either chain and the Decision Score collapses to a monitoring output. Retain the cross-module architecture and the output becomes an actionable decision with a defensible causal attribution.

ES — Español

Los dos estudios de caso de prueba de concepto principales de GROAN no se seleccionan arbitrariamente. Se eligen porque cada uno demuestra un argumento estructural específico: que las señales químicas entre ecosistemas, procesadas a través del Motor de Interacción Cruzada de Módulos, producen decisiones que los marcos de monitoreo de ecosistema único son estructuralmente incapaces de generar. El caso del Caribe prueba la capacidad del CMIE para la atribución causal terrestre-a-arrecife. El caso del Mar Rojo prueba la capacidad del CMIE para la detección de amplificación térmica-química a través de límites de ecosistemas.

Estudio de Caso 01 · Caribe · Datos CORDAP

Los bosques de manglares en el Caribe sirven como fuentes críticas de alcalinidad. La respiración de las raíces y la descomposición de la materia orgánica exportan agua rica en bicarbonato y alta alcalinidad hacia las zonas de arrecife adyacentes, amortiguando el sistema de carbonatos local contra la acidificación metabólica diurna. Cuando la cobertura de manglares se contrae, la alcalinidad total (AT) disminuye 30–80 µmol/kg en el penacho de descarga de la temporada lluviosa, suprimiendo Ωarag desde más de 3.0 a la zona de peligro de 1.8–2.2. Solo CMIE, recibiendo datos concurrentes de pérdida de cobertura GMIN, métricas de disminución de peces juveniles GMAIN y señales de supresión de carbonatos GRIN, puede generar la atribución causal correcta: la pérdida de manglares es el impulsor principal.

Estudio de Caso 02 · Mar Rojo · Datos KAUST

El caso del Mar Rojo demuestra una vía de amplificación térmica-química. Los praderas de pastos marinos en el Mar Rojo realizan una función de amortiguación del carbonato análoga a los manglares en el Caribe. La firma química de un prado de pastos marinos saludable es una amplitud diurna de OD >3 mg/L y una señal de pCO₂ diurna suprimida. Cuando la TSM supera 1°C por encima de la media mensual máxima (MMM) durante cuatro o más semanas consecutivas, la actividad metabólica de los pastos marinos se desacelera. La consecuencia química es diagnóstica: la amplitud diurna del OD se comprime por debajo de 2 mg/L, el pCO₂ aumenta y la amortiguación diurna de Ω desaparece. El riesgo de blanqueamiento es una amplificación multiplicativa — no la suma del estrés térmico más el estrés de acidificación.

Argumento clave: Ambos estudios de caso producen la misma prueba estructural. Los parámetros químicos no son la contribución. La contribución es la cadena causal que CMIE construye a través de los límites del ecosistema usando esos parámetros. Elimine cualquier módulo de cualquiera de las cadenas y el Puntaje de Decisión colapsa a una salida de monitoreo. Mantenga la arquitectura de módulo cruzado y la salida se convierte en una decisión accionable con una atribución causal defendible.

Application
EN — English
  • 01When GMIN detects mangrove coverage loss exceeding 10% in a reef-adjacent watershed, pre-apply an alkalinity buffering deficit correction to downstream MOOP carbonate data before normalization. Raw TA values from deficit zones will understate the buffering loss and produce a false elevated Ω reading in CMIE.
  • 02In the Red Sea theater, SST anomaly exceeding 1°C above MMM for four or more consecutive weeks triggers a GSIN metabolic stress watch. Begin daily DO diel amplitude monitoring at all seagrass-adjacent MOOP stations immediately — compression of amplitude below 2 mg/L is the early chemical warning of buffering collapse before GRIN bleaching signal appears.
  • 03Never apply Caribbean TA or pCO₂ endmember values to Red Sea stations. Red Sea baseline alkalinity and dissolved inorganic carbon concentrations differ materially from the Caribbean due to higher evaporation, lower freshwater input, and distinct biological carbonate production rates. Using generic ocean endmembers in CMIE Red Sea calculations will produce systematic attribution errors.
  • 04When CMIE constructs a cross-ecosystem causal chain spanning three or more modules — as in both case studies — the Decision Score must include a cross-module confidence weight derived from the weakest-confidence input in the chain. The DS is only as defensible as its lowest-confidence link. Document the chain and the confidence floor in the CMIE calibration record.
  • 05The intervention target generated by CMIE in the Caribbean case — the mangrove watershed, not the reef — must be explicitly stated in the DMAP-CAL™ output alongside the reef chemical data. Stakeholders presenting the reef data without the watershed attribution will misidentify the intervention point and waste resources treating symptoms rather than the causal driver.

Las partes interesadas que presenten los datos del arrecife sin la atribución de la cuenca identificarán incorrectamente el punto de intervención y desperdiciarán recursos tratando síntomas en lugar del impulsor causal.
