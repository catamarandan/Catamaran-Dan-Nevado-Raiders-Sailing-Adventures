Section 02 · Chemical Oceanography · L20 / 32

Regional Systems

Theater-specific carbonate baselines, forcing regimes, and CMIE correction protocols

Lecture
EN — English

GROAN operates across four distinct chemical oceanographic regions, each with a unique carbonate baseline, dominant forcing regime, and set of required corrections before data enters the CMIE pipeline. Regional chemistry is not background noise. It is the reference frame against which every anomaly is measured, every threshold applied, and every Decision Score calculated. Using the wrong regional baseline is not a minor calibration error — it is a systematic attribution failure that will misdirect intervention resources across entire countries.

Region 01 · Caribbean
Ω 3.0–3.8 TA ~2300 34–36 PSU 26–30 °C

The Caribbean is chemically defined by its terrestrial connectivity. Seasonal mangrove-sourced alkalinity subsidies, agricultural DIN loading, and submarine groundwater discharge create reef chemistry that fluctuates by ±80–120 µmol/kg TA between dry and wet seasons. The Rim Run™ operational circuit traverses this theater from Mexico to Panama, covering 7 countries and 100+ reef sites. Ωarag baseline is among the highest in GROAN theaters, but the buffering capacity is fragile and erosion-prone. CORDAP provides the primary carbonate record. Dry-season baselines must not be applied to wet-season data — the seasonal TA swing is large enough to invert anomaly polarity in CMIE.

Region 02 · Red Sea
Ω 3.5–4.2 TA ~2450 38–42 PSU 22–32 °C

The Red Sea is a semi-enclosed hypersaline basin with minimal freshwater input and extreme evaporative concentration. Baseline total alkalinity of ~2450 µmol/kg — approximately 150 µmol/kg above the global ocean average — produces naturally elevated Ωarag. This is a structural feature, not a sign of exceptional reef health. The key vulnerability is thermal: Red Sea corals live near their upper thermal tolerance limit, and SST anomalies above 1°C MMM for four-plus weeks trigger measurable seagrass metabolic suppression before reef bleaching signals appear. Red Sea Research Center provides the authoritative baseline. Generic ocean endmembers will produce a ~0.3 Ω unit systematic overestimate of acidification severity.

Region 03 · Coral Triangle
Ω 3.2–3.9 TA ~2280 33–35 PSU 27–30 °C

The Coral Triangle — encompassing Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Papua New Guinea, Solomon Islands, and Timor-Leste — hosts the highest marine biodiversity on Earth and the most chemically complex water mass environment in GROAN. The Indonesian Throughflow transports approximately 15 Sv of Pacific water into the Indian Ocean through the archipelago, creating active mixing of distinct water masses with different TA, DIC, and nutrient signatures within 50 km of reef sites. CTI (Coral Triangle Initiative) partnership data supports this theater as GROAN Blue Rim 5™ Chapter 2. CMIE multi-stressor integration is most critical here: thermal, chemical, fishing pressure, and crown-of-thorns signals must be attributed independently before cross-module combination.

Region 04 · Eastern Pacific
Ω 1.5–3.2 TA ~2250 33–35 PSU 20–29 °C

The Eastern Pacific presents GROAN's most analytically demanding chemical environment. The Costa Rica Dome and Panama Bight are major upwelling centers that bring cold, CO₂-rich, low-pH water to the surface seasonally, naturally suppressing Ωarag to 1.5–2.0 — values that would trigger High-priority CMIE alerts in any other theater. ENSO state governs interannual variability: El Niño years suppress upwelling and warm the surface, while La Niña years intensify upwelling and depress Ω further. The critical CMIE protocol for this theater is mandatory upwelling index correction — without it, naturally low Ω will be systematically misattributed to anthropogenic ocean acidification, and CORDAP-funded interventions will be misdirected.

Cross-regional rule: ENSO state must be documented in every CMIE calibration record for Pacific and Caribbean theater analyses. El Niño and La Niña systematically shift regional baseline chemistry — Caribbean precipitation increases in La Niña years elevate DIN loading; Eastern Pacific Ω drops further during La Niña upwelling intensification. An undocumented ENSO state is a confounding variable in every cross-module attribution CMIE performs in these theaters.

ES — Español

GROAN opera en cuatro regiones oceanográficas químicas distintas, cada una con una línea base de carbonatos única, un régimen de forzamiento dominante y un conjunto de correcciones requeridas antes de que los datos ingresen al pipeline de CMIE. La química regional no es ruido de fondo. Es el marco de referencia contra el cual se mide cada anomalía, se aplica cada umbral y se calcula cada Puntaje de Decisión. Usar la línea base regional incorrecta no es un error menor de calibración — es una falla de atribución sistemática que desviará los recursos de intervención en países enteros.

Caribe: Definido por su conectividad terrestre. Las subsidios de alcalinidad estacionales de los manglares y la carga de DIN agrícola crean una química de arrecife que fluctúa en ±80–120 µmol/kg AT entre las temporadas seca y lluviosa. El circuito operacional Rim Run™ atraviesa este teatro de México a Panamá. Las líneas base de temporada seca no deben aplicarse a datos de temporada lluviosa.

Mar Rojo: Cuenca hipersalina semicerrada con AT de referencia de ~2450 µmol/kg — aproximadamente 150 µmol/kg por encima del promedio global. Los valores oceánicos genéricos producirán una sobreestimación sistemática de ~0.3 unidades de Ω de la severidad de acidificación. El proporciona la línea base autorizada.

Triángulo de Coral: Alberga la mayor biodiversidad marina del mundo. El Pasaje Oceánico de Indonesia transporta aproximadamente 15 Sv de agua del Pacífico al Océano Índico, creando mezcla activa de masas de agua distintas con diferentes firmas de AT, CID y nutrientes. La integración multi-estresor del CMIE es más crítica aquí.

Pacífico Oriental: El entorno químico más exigente analíticamente de GROAN. La Cúpula de Costa Rica y la Bahía de Panamá son centros de surgencia que suprimen naturalmente Ωarag a 1.5–2.0. La corrección del índice de surgencia es obligatoria — sin ella, el Ω naturalmente bajo se atribuirá sistemáticamente de forma incorrecta a la acidificación oceánica antropogénica.

Regla transregional: El estado de ENSO debe documentarse en cada registro de calibración CMIE para los análisis de los teatros del Pacífico y el Caribe. Un estado de ENSO no documentado es una variable de confusión en cada atribución de módulo cruzado que realiza CMIE en estos teatros.

Application
EN — English
  • 01In the Caribbean theater, never compare MOOP carbonate samples collected in the dry season (December–April) against a wet-season baseline, or vice versa. The ±80–120 µmol/kg TA seasonal swing is large enough to invert anomaly polarity in CMIE — a dry-season reading assessed against a wet-season mean will appear artificially elevated, producing a false High-priority alert.
  • 02In the Red Sea theater, never substitute generic ocean TA (~2300 µmol/kg) for the Red Sea baseline (~2450 µmol/kg). The 150 µmol/kg difference will produce a systematic ~0.3 Ω unit overestimate of acidification severity, generating false High-priority CMIE alerts and misdirecting intervention resources. Use Red Sea Research Center baseline values exclusively.
  • 03In the Coral Triangle theater, any MOOP station located within 50 km of the Indonesian Throughflow corridor requires water mass endmember identification before carbonate data enters the normalization pipeline. Throughflow mixing produces intermediate TA and DIC values that do not correspond to either Pacific or Indian Ocean endmembers — assign a Moderate confidence floor until the water mass origin is established.
  • 04In the Eastern Pacific theater, apply an upwelling index correction before any Ω value enters CMIE for attribution analysis. During La Niña upwelling intensification, naturally occurring Ω of 1.5–2.0 will trigger automatic High-priority alerts if uncorrected. The correction protocol must distinguish natural upwelling suppression from anthropogenic acidification trend before a Decision Score is generated.
  • 05For all Pacific and Caribbean theater analyses, document the current ENSO state (El Niño / Neutral / La Niña and ONI index value) in the CMIE calibration record at the time of data ingestion. An undocumented ENSO state cannot be retrospectively corrected and will leave a systematic confounding variable in the attribution record. This is a mandatory metadata field — not optional documentation.
