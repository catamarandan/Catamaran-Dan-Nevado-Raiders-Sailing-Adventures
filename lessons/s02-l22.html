Corridor Integration

Rim Run™ transect chemistry — 7 countries, temporal aliasing, satellite anchoring, and annual repeatability

Lecture
EN — English

The Rim Run™ expedition — a zero-carbon annual sailing circuit from Mexico to Panama aboard Shamrocket — is not simply a field sampling program. It is GROAN's primary in-situ chemical transect instrument: a 1,500+ nautical mile, 7-country, 100+ reef-site corridor that generates the ground-truth carbonate chemistry data against which satellite fields are calibrated and CMIE cross-module attributions are anchored. Integrating chemistry collected along this corridor into a coherent GROAN dataset requires solving four distinct analytical problems that do not arise in fixed-station monitoring programs.

Problem 01 · Temporal Aliasing

A Mexico reef station sampled in October and a Panama reef station sampled in December are not contemporaneous observations — they are separated by up to eight weeks of evolving ocean conditions. Direct comparison without temporal correction will conflate seasonal chemistry with spatial chemistry, producing spurious longitudinal gradients in CMIE. The correction protocol: anchor each corridor sample to the GLORYS reanalysis field at the date and location of collection, compute the anomaly from the reanalysis baseline, and use anomalies — not raw values — for cross-country chemical comparison. Raw values are retained for country-specific CMIE calibration records only.

Problem 02 · Satellite Anchoring

MOOP corridor stations are point measurements separated by 15 km minimum spacing for valid kriging. Between stations, chemistry is inferred from satellite-derived fields — GLORYS SST/salinity, Sentinel-2 CDOM and turbidity proxies. Each MOOP station anchors a 15 km kriging radius in the satellite spatial field. For anchoring to be valid, each station must be logged with GPS position and UTC timestamp so it can be retrospectively aligned with the nearest Sentinel-2 overpass. A station without a confirmed overpass alignment within ±3 days is treated as an unanchored point — it contributes to the MOOP record but cannot upgrade satellite-derived estimates from Moderate to High confidence in its zone.

Problem 03 · Cross-Country Transitions

At each national boundary along the corridor, baseline chemistry, correction protocols, and data gap profiles change simultaneously. A 25 km buffer zone on each side of every national boundary is designated as a transition zone in CMIE — stations within this buffer receive country-specific corrections applied from both adjacent national baselines, and the resulting DS carries an elevated uncertainty flag. The transition zone prevents CMIE from generating a hard attribution boundary at a political line that has no corresponding chemical discontinuity in the ocean. The Belize–Guatemala boundary near the Gulf of Honduras is the most chemically complex transition in the corridor — riverine discharge from the Motagua River creates a persistent turbid plume that crosses the boundary seasonally.

Problem 04 · Annual Repeatability

The Rim Run™ annual circuit means each reef site is re-sampled once per year — the temporal resolution of GROAN's corridor chemistry record is one year per site. For CMIE trend detection to be valid, consecutive-year samples at the same site must be collected within ±2 hours of the same tidal phase and ±2 hours of the same time of day. Carbonate chemistry varies by 0.1–0.3 pH units and 0.3–0.5 Ω units within a single tidal cycle at reef sites — a sampling phase mismatch of four hours can exceed the annual trend signal GROAN is designed to detect. Tidal phase matching is not a best practice. It is a mandatory metadata constraint for all corridor sites in CMIE trend analysis.

The longitudinal gradient. The corridor's chemical character shifts systematically from north to south. Mexico and Belize are karst-dominated — SGD suppresses Ω and elevates DIN against an otherwise high-carbonate Caribbean background. Guatemala and Honduras are riverine-dominated — the Motagua and Ulúa river systems deliver turbid, nutrient-loaded freshwater that depresses Ω and creates the corridor's largest DIN plume. Nicaragua is the corridor's chemical refuge — low anthropogenic pressure, minimal riverine loading, and Ω values approaching the corridor maximum. Costa Rica marks the Pacific transition — the Gulf of Nicoya introduces cooler, lower-pH water from Pacific influence, and Raising Coral NGO operations here provide the only independent verification data point in the southern corridor. Panama closes the circuit at the upwelling boundary — Gulf of Panama upwelling can drive Ω as low as 1.8 seasonally, the lowest in the corridor and the most analytically demanding CMIE attribution environment outside the Eastern Pacific theater.

Key argument: No satellite product, model reanalysis, or fixed-station network can replicate what the Rim Run™ corridor provides — a continuous, annually repeated, zero-carbon, human-verified chemical transect across seven national jurisdictions at a spatial resolution and reef-zone specificity that no other data collection method achieves at this scale. The corridor is not supporting evidence for GROAN. It is the ground-truth layer without which CMIE cross-border attribution is unverifiable.

ES — Español

La expedición Rim Run™ — un circuito de navegación anual de cero carbono de México a Panamá a bordo de Shamrocket — es el instrumento primario de transecto químico in situ de GROAN: un corredor de 1,500+ millas náuticas, 7 países, 100+ sitios de arrecife que genera los datos de química de carbonatos de verdad sobre el terreno contra los cuales se calibran los campos satelitales y se anclan las atribuciones de módulo cruzado del CMIE. Integrar la química recolectada a lo largo de este corredor en un conjunto de datos GROAN coherente requiere resolver cuatro problemas analíticos distintos.

Aliasing Temporal: Una estación arrecifal de México muestreada en octubre y una de Panamá muestreada en diciembre no son observaciones contemporáneas — están separadas por hasta ocho semanas de condiciones oceánicas en evolución. El protocolo de corrección: ancle cada muestra del corredor al campo de reanálisis GLORYS en la fecha y ubicación de la recolección, calcule la anomalía desde la línea base de reanálisis y use anomalías — no valores brutos — para la comparación química entre países.

Anclaje Satelital: Las estaciones del corredor MOOP son mediciones puntuales separadas por un espaciado mínimo de 15 km para kriging válido. Cada estación MOOP ancla un radio de kriging de 15 km en el campo espacial satelital. Para que el anclaje sea válido, cada estación debe registrarse con posición GPS y marca de tiempo UTC para alinearse con el sobrevuelo Sentinel-2 más cercano.

Transiciones Entre Países: Una zona de amortiguación de 25 km en cada lado de cada frontera nacional se designa como zona de transición en CMIE. Las estaciones dentro de este buffer reciben correcciones de línea base específicas del país aplicadas desde ambas líneas base nacionales adyacentes. La frontera Belice–Guatemala es la transición química más compleja del corredor — la descarga fluvial del Río Motagua crea un penacho turbio persistente que cruza la frontera estacionalmente.

Repetibilidad Anual: Las muestras en años consecutivos en el mismo sitio deben recolectarse dentro de ±2 horas de la misma fase de marea y ±2 horas de la misma hora del día. La química de carbonatos varía 0.1–0.3 unidades de pH y 0.3–0.5 unidades de Ω dentro de un solo ciclo de marea — un desajuste de fase de muestreo de cuatro horas puede superar la señal de tendencia anual. La concordancia de fase de marea es una restricción de metadatos obligatoria.

Argumento clave: Ningún producto satelital, reanálisis de modelo o red de estaciones fijas puede replicar lo que proporciona el corredor Rim Run™ — un transecto químico continuo, repetido anualmente, de cero carbono y verificado humanamente en siete jurisdicciones nacionales. El corredor es la capa de verdad terreno sin la cual la atribución transfronteriza del CMIE es inverificable.

Application
EN — English
  • 01Never compare raw carbonate values across corridor countries collected at different expedition dates. Compute the anomaly from the GLORYS reanalysis field at the collection date and location for each sample, and use anomalies for cross-country comparison. The temporal aliasing error between an October Mexico sample and a December Panama sample can exceed the actual longitudinal chemical gradient CMIE is designed to detect.
  • 02Each MOOP corridor station must be logged with GPS position and UTC timestamp at collection. Match each station to the nearest Sentinel-2 overpass within ±3 days. A station without a confirmed overpass alignment cannot upgrade satellite-derived CDOM or turbidity proxies in its 15 km kriging zone from Moderate to High confidence — the satellite field remains uncalibrated at that location regardless of instrument precision.
  • 03Apply the 25 km transition zone buffer at every national boundary in the corridor. The Belize–Guatemala boundary near the Gulf of Honduras requires particular care — the Motagua River plume crosses this boundary seasonally, and DIN values on the Belize side during the wet season may reflect Guatemalan agricultural discharge rather than Belizean conditions. CMIE must not attribute elevated DIN to the country of measurement without plume tracking confirmation.
  • 04At annual re-sampling, collect each site within ±2 hours of the previous year's tidal phase and ±2 hours of the previous year's time of day. If the tidal window cannot be matched within this constraint, flag the sample as phase-uncertain and assign Moderate confidence for CMIE trend analysis. A four-hour tidal phase mismatch produces a carbonate chemistry range that can exceed the inter-annual trend signal — the trend detection is invalidated, not merely degraded.
  • 05Panama corridor stations in the Gulf of Panama upwelling zone require the same upwelling index correction mandatory in the Eastern Pacific theater — Ω values of 1.8–2.0 at Panama sites during upwelling season are natural, not anthropogenic. CMIE must not generate a High-priority acidification alert for Panama upwelling-suppressed Ω without first confirming that the upwelling index correction has been applied. Failing this check will produce the highest false-positive rate in the entire Rim Run™ corridor.
